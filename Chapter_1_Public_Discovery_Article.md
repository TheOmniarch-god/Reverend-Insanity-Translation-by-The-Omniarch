# Chapter 1: Though the Flesh Perish, the Demonic Heart Knows No Regret
### A Discovery & Lore Breakdown — What Chapter 1 Really Tells Us

> **Public Reader's Guide — No spoilers beyond Chapter 1. No editorial notes. Just what the text actually gives you if you read slowly.**

---

### 1. The Cold Open: We Start At The End

Reverend Insanity doesn't start with a farm boy dreaming of adventure. It starts with a funeral.

Fang Yuan is already dead. Surrounded on a blood-soaked mountaintop by the entire Righteous Path, hundreds of wounds, a pool of blood at his feet, and still no one dares to step forward. They have laid an **inescapable dragnet** (天罗地网) — not just a net, a military-grade encirclement. For six hours they just stare at him.

That's our first discovery: **In this world, one man can make the righteous world afraid to move.** Even dying, Fang Yuan has that weight.

He doesn't beg. He recites poetry, laughs, and self-detonates.

Then we cut to rain.

That structure — apocalypse, then quiet village rain — tells you everything about Gu Zhen Ren's storytelling: grand, then intimate. Five hundred years of history collapsed into one explosion.

### 2. Huaxia — Why Not Just "China"?

> "He had originally been a Huaxia student on Earth..."

This is one of the most important words in Chapter 1, and most translations flatten it to "Chinese."

**Huaxia (华夏)** is not the modern political name Zhongguo (中国). It's the oldest poetic name for the Chinese civilization itself.

- **Hua (华)** = splendid, luminous, flowery — culture, ritual, beauty
- **Xia (夏)** = grand, great — the Xia dynasty, the first dynasty

Together, Huaxia means **"the splendid and grand civilization."** It's how Chinese people referred to themselves for 3,000 years before modern borders existed. It conveys *cultural belonging*, not a passport.

When Gu Zhen Ren writes that Fang Yuan is from Huaxia, he is saying:
1. Fang Yuan is not just a modern kid — he carries 5,000 years of cultural memory
2. His poetry, his philosophy, his ruthlessness is rooted in that lineage
3. He is a transmigrator, but not a blank slate — he is *culturally* Chinese to the bone

That's why in the next paragraph he can quote Li Bai and Yang Shen without thinking. Huaxia explains his literary soul.

For us translating, we kept "Huaxia" in early drafts, then settled on "Chinese" with a note, but the discovery is: **Fang Yuan's identity is civilizational, not national.**

### 3. Spring Autumn Cicada — Time Is A Gu

The object everyone wants: **Spring Autumn Cicada (春秋蝉)**.

Chapter 1 tells us three things that will define the whole novel:

- It's **Rank 7**, and one of the **Ten Great Mystical Gu**. In a world where most Gu Masters never touch Rank 5, Rank 7 is mythic. The fact that Fang Yuan refined it explains why the whole world came to kill him.
- Its ability: **reverse time**. Not time travel — reversal. It sends the will back.
- Its cost: Fang Yuan slaughtered tens of millions to refine it. That's not hyperbole in this universe — that's the Demonic Path's accounting.

The name itself is a discovery: **Spring and Autumn** refers to the Spring and Autumn Annals (春秋), the ancient Chinese chronicle where time was first recorded as moral history. A cicada that lives through spring and autumn — short life, but it *hears* history. Gu Zhen Ren loves this kind of naming: the Gu *is* its philosophy.

Fang Yuan's last line before exploding: "If this newly refined Spring Autumn Cicada works, I will still be a demon in my next life!"

That's not a villain monologue. That's a vow. He chooses the Demonic Path again, knowing the cost.

### 4. The Poem He Recites Before Dying

> Green mountains beneath the setting sun,
> Autumn moon and spring breeze.
> In the morning one's hair is like black silk,
> By dusk it turns to snow.
> Right and wrong, success and failure,
> All vanish in a turn of the head.

Two lines are borrowed, and that's deliberate:

- **"In the morning one's hair is like black silk, by dusk it turns to snow" (朝如青丝暮成雪)** — from Li Bai, the immortal poet. About how fast life passes.
- **"Right and wrong, success and failure, all vanish in a turn of the head" (是非成败转头空)** — from Yang Shen's *Lin Jiang Xian*, the poem that opens *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*. It's literally the opening line of China's most famous historical novel about ambition and emptiness.

Fang Yuan, a Huaxia student, quotes the two ultimate poems about **impermanence** as he dies. He is not sad. He is *summarizing* his 500 years: black hair to white, success to dust, in one turn of the head.

That tells you: this is a story about time, not power.

### 5. What Is The Demonic Path, Actually?

Chapter 1 gives us the only definition we need, from Fang Yuan's inner voice:

> "For what was the Demonic Path? It was to accumulate no good karma, only murder and arson; spurned by heaven and reviled by earth, making enemies with the whole world, and still running wild, following the whim of one's own heart to the very end."

Most xianxia says demonic = evil. Gu Zhen Ren says demonic = **freedom at absolute cost**. No good karma, no heaven's favor, enemies everywhere, and you still do it.

And crucially: **"A trace of wistfulness stirred, yet not the faintest whisper of regret."**

That's the title: Though the flesh perish, the demonic heart knows no regret. The body can rot, the reputation can be damned, but the choice is not regretted. That's not edgy — that's existential.

### 6. Qing Mao Mountain & Gu Yue Village — The World After Rebirth

After the explosion, we wake up in rain.

**Qing Mao Mountain (青茅山)** — Green Thatch Mountain. Not a grand sacred mountain, a thatch-covered hill. Humble.

**Gu Yue Village (古月村)** — Ancient Moon Village. Gu Yue is also the clan name. Notice: Gu (蛊) + Yue (月) = Gu + Moon. The clan that raises Gu under the moon.

Details Chapter 1 quietly gives you:

- **Several thousand households, not ten thousand** — the narrator corrects himself. This is a small clan, not a city. Intimate, poor, political.
- **Stilt houses** with lights in the rain — southern Chinese mountain architecture, not northern palaces
- **Amber floorboards, black-lacquered altar, red-copper incense burners** — the ancestral hall is tactile. You can smell the incense.
- **Kowtow** — foreheads thudding softly. Ritual matters here.

And the **Aperture Opening Ceremony** — the moment every 15-year-old opens their primeval essence sea. This is the entire cultivation system's foundation, and Chapter 1 treats it as a village sacrament, not a magic academy. Prayers, not lectures.

### 7. The Aptitude System — Hope and Cruelty

Chapter 1 introduces the talent system that will drive Fang Yuan's life:

- **Grade A, B, C, D** — not just letters, a caste. Three years without a Grade A is a crisis.
- **Bai Ning Bing** — mentioned only in gossip, but already terrifying: Grade A, Rank 3 in two years. The benchmark genius. The elders fear him by name.
- **Fang Yuan's reputation** — Could speak at 3 months, walk at 4, compose *Bring in the Wine* (Li Bai), *Ode to the Plum Blossom*, *Jiang Cheng Zi* (Su Shi) by age 5. The village thinks he's a future Grade A.

Discovery: **The village values poetry as proof of cultivation talent.** In Huaxia culture, precocious literary talent = spiritual talent. That's why his poems matter. And that's why the elders immediately start plotting to recruit him — orphans with no parents are political assets. "If one could bring him into their own faction and groom him well, it could guarantee a century of prosperity for their line!"

Chapter 1 is not just rebirth — it's a political thriller about child prodigies.

### 8. The Rebirth Itself — Five Hundred Years

Fang Yuan's first words after rebirth:

> "Gu Yue Village... this is five hundred years ago?! The Spring Autumn Cicada really had effect..."

He looks at his young, pale palm, clenches it, listens to rain on the window lattice, and says:

> "Five hundred years of experience... it truly feels like a dream."

Yet he knows it's not a dream.

What did we learn? He has **500 years of memory** — 300 years drifting, 200 years as a Demon Lord. He is 15 in body, 500+ in mind. Every other 15-year-old is worrying about aptitude. He is worrying about how to not waste his second life.

And the last line of Chapter 1 is pure Gu Zhen Ren: wistful, but certain.

### 9. So What Is Chapter 1 Really About?

If you had to summarize Chapter 1 in one discovery, it's this:

**Chapter 1 is about a man who had everything, lost it, and chose the same path again — but earlier.**

It's not about getting a second chance to be good. It's about getting a second chance to be *more efficiently* yourself.

Huaxia explains his soul. Spring Autumn Cicada explains his method. The poem explains his worldview. Gu Yue Village explains his starting point. And the elders' gossip explains the world that will try to use him.

That's why Chapter 1 is called "Though the Flesh Perish, the Demonic Heart Knows No Regret." The flesh perished on that mountaintop. The heart didn't.
