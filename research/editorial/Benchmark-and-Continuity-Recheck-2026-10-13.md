# Benchmark report verification and continuity recheck

**For The Omniarch | 13 October 2026**

## Verdict

The Chapter 22 C-grade/38% inconsistency is confirmed in three checked textual witnesses. Its exact repair is not established. Crucially, surrounding context makes a simple C-to-D substitution less satisfactory than it first appeared.

The attached benchmark report contains many good proposed corrections, but **its claim that the corrections are live is not verified and conflicts with the public pages retrieved in this check**. The old wording remains in the checked locations. This is a distinction between the quality of the proposed revisions and their publication status, not a claim about what exists in the translator’s local files.

No translation or original report has been modified.

## 2. Chapter 22: what actually conflicts

### The disputed statement

Volume 1, Chapter 22, 月刃飞舞, EPUB `chapter30.html`:

> 月刃的消耗太大了，我只是丙等修为，空窍中能存储三成八的青铜真元。只能连续催发出三片月刃。

The student describes C-grade status, a maximum storage of 38%, and only three consecutive moonblades.

Volume 1, Chapter 91, EPUB `chapter99.html`, explicitly sets C-grade limits:

> 就拿丙等而言，空窍中真元的存储极限，最少是四成，最多能达到五成九。

That means a minimum of 40% and a maximum of 59%. This is not merely an inference from the abbreviated early grade table.

### Witness comparison

| Witness | Reading found | Evidential limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Supplied EPUB | 丙等 / 三成八 / 三片 | Designated base, not an authenticated author manuscript. |
| Zhihu early-chapter text | 丙等 / 三成八 / 三片 | Same substantive reading; textual independence not established. |
| Hetushu Chapter 22 HTML | 丙等 / 三成八 / 三片 | Direct HTML confirms the sentence; extraction order is scrambled. |
| Baidu Baike Gu article | Quotes Chapter 7’s C-grade 四五成 and D-grade 两三成 | Corroborates the general scheme, but supplies no correction of this particular sentence. |

Sources: [Zhihu witness](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/609481302); [Hetushu Chapter 22](https://www.hetushu.com/book/7640/5345261.html); [2](https://baike.baidu.com/item/%E8%9B%8A/13877051).

Three matching witnesses demonstrate circulation of the reading, not three independent confirmations of its correctness. No authenticated corrected reading was located.

### Why changing C to D is not automatically the best repair

Earlier in the same scene, 35 of the 57 students have chosen and refined Moonlight Gu. The other 22 are explicitly described as D-grade students who abandoned that choice because of their aptitude:

> 其余的少年，都是丁等资质，不是不想炼化月光蛊，而是资质不行，只能知难而退。

The 22 watch from outside the exercise. The complaining student is presented among the students who have stopped practising moonblades.

Changing only 丙 to 丁 repairs the grade/percentage mismatch but puts a D-grade practitioner into a scene that has just separated those students from the exercise. It is therefore not a clean one-character solution when the whole scene is considered.

This corrects the emphasis of my earlier suggestion. C-to-D remains a conceivable intervention, but should not be preferred merely because it changes fewer characters.

### Candidate repairs

| Candidate | What it fixes | What it changes or leaves unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| C → D; retain 38% and three shots | The percentage band and shot arithmetic agree. | Conflicts with the scene’s separation of D-grade students from the exercise. |
| 38% → 48%; three shots → four | Preserves the C-grade participant and makes the basic 10%-per-shot arithmetic consistent. | Two numerical emendations; no checked witness gives this wording. 48% is plausible, not recoverable as certain authorial intent. |
| Treat 38% as current remaining essence | Could allow a C-grade student to have three shots left. | The source says what the aperture can store, not what remains. Requires rewriting the proposition, not just interpreting it. |
| Widen C-grade downward | Preserves this isolated line. | Contradicts the explicit later definition and damages the wider system. Not recommended. |

**Recommendation:** record a confirmed source inconsistency. If you approve a coherence-focused emendation, C-grade with 48% and four consecutive shots is currently the more scene-consistent candidate, but it must be labelled internally as conjectural, not as a reading found in another edition.

Possible prose, only if approved:

> “Moonblades use up too much essence. With my Grade C aptitude, my aperture can hold only forty-eight percent bronze primeval essence. That is enough for just four moonblades in succession.”

“修为” is also imprecise here because the A–D scheme concerns aptitude, not cultivation revolution. “Grade C aptitude” is the sensible English rendering of the intended category.

### Chapter 23 is related, but not equally conclusive

Chapter 23, `chapter31.html`, says a C-grade Gu Master’s essence is nearly exhausted after three or four attacks. The qualifier “nearly” and unspecified starting reserves make this less exact than the Chapter 22 maximum-capacity claim. Do not automatically rewrite that sentence into a strict full-reserve shot-count table.

## 3. What Baidu Baike did and did not establish

The general 蛊真人 address opened the author’s biography, not a critical edition of the novel. The useful lore page was the Gu article’s 修炼资质 section. It quotes the familiar Chapter 7 aptitude account, including D-grade 两三成 and C-grade 四五成. [2](https://baike.baidu.com/item/%E8%9B%8A/13877051)

This supports the ordinary grade scheme but does not decide which word or number is corrupt in Chapter 22.

There was an important retrieval warning: the search result associated with the Primeval Essence page supplied an elaborate account involving numbered essence particles, while the directly fetched page was only a brief terminology stub. I could not verify that elaborate account in the opened page, so it is excluded from the evidence. My interim remark about conflicting Baike descriptions should be narrowed accordingly: **the search result and the retrieved page disagreed; I did not establish that both descriptions are present on the current pages.**

The Gu page also gives an approximate 120-green-grape operating-cost example. Our earlier primary-source passages at Volume 5, Chapters 181 and 185 specify at least 130 in their stated context. Such secondary summaries must be checked against the specific passages before they become numerical policy.

**Source hierarchy:** base text and explicit scene context first; alternate textual witnesses second; encyclopedias as leads and corroboration, not automatic overrides.

### C8-04: better integrated wording

The report’s inserted passage restores the missing material, but changes tense between narrated recollection and direct thought and contains an em dash contrary to its own stated policy.

Suggested version:

> Except in special circumstances, novice Gu Masters chose a First Revolution Gu for their first refinement. Fang Yuan had hoped to obtain the Flower Wine Monk’s Liquor Worm and refine it as his vital Gu. But he still had no clue where the monk’s remains lay. He did not know when he would find them, or whether someone else would find them first. To be safe, he decided to choose a Moonlight Gu.

This is a proposed rendering, not a claim that it is already in the live text. Direct interior thought can be retained instead if that better matches the chapter’s established style.

### C8-10: preserve the image without moving the time qualifier

The source gives the sudden transition from darkness into light, then separately says that waking each day feels like a beautiful dream. The report attaches “Each day upon waking” to the first image, slightly rearranging that relationship.

Prefer:

> He felt as though he had suddenly stepped out of a dark corner into a paradise filled with light. Each morning when he woke, it seemed he was living a beautiful dream.

### C5-01: avoid unnecessary doubling

The essential repair is explicit killing with a clear referent. An economical option:

> With Strength and Wisdom together, he killed more Predicaments, eating their flesh and drinking their blood to survive.

The final line should be fitted to the full surrounding Chinese paragraph rather than substituted mechanically.

### C7-02: lexical choice and narrative perspective

The report is right that a literal “flower thief” may fail to communicate the sexual meaning to English readers. But “raw” versus “euphemistic” is not the only issue: this chapter recounts the clan’s received account. Later revelations about the conflict must not be flattened by an editorial statement that everything the clan says has been objectively verified.

“Flower Wine Monk” is an established glossary choice. 行者 is not universally equivalent to a formally ordained monk in every context; avoid turning this chosen name into a universal dictionary rule.

## 6. Internal problems in the fixes report

1. **Counts do not reconcile.** It opens with 11 P1/P2 fixes, closes with 22, and contains 25 distinct benchmark IDs. Grouped operations may explain some difference, but no counting method is supplied. Use one row per ID and separate proposed/applied/verified counts.
2. **The em-dash claim contradicts the quoted revisions.** C8-04 includes “find them [em dash] or whether…”, and C8-09 includes “feel [em dash] wonderful…”. Neither is an abrupt dialogue interruption. Replace these in the proposed text, not merely in the policy statement.
3. **The 买醉 status is internally superseded.** An early section says “senseless” remains live; a later section says “get drunk” was applied. The currently retrieved public page has neither: it still says “indulge in wine”. Keep a single final status entry.
4. **Verification language is too broad.** “All identified P1 issues fixed and verified … where available” leaves unclear which base passages were actually checked and which output files were tested.
5. **“No tampering else” is not auditable from this report.** That claim requires a file diff or versioned before/after comparison. I have not certified design, mobile layout, or all 16 claimed files.
6. **British spelling is not visibly complete in the retrieved text.** Chapter 2 still has “grueling” and “harbored”. A UK house style may choose -ise, while recognising that Oxford -ize is also British. State the house choice without treating all -ize forms as American errors.

Suggested release record fields: ID, Chinese location, old text, approved replacement, source-file version, public URL/anchor, observed public text, verification date, status.

## 7. Actual remaining inconsistencies and their priority

### A. Confirmed source inconsistency: C-grade / 38%

High confidence, now cross-witness confirmed. Repair remains conjectural for the reasons above.

### B. Strong source chronology conflict: Chapter 8

The base EPUB itself contains:

- Chapter 7 opens with one week having passed.
- The elder refers to the ceremony seven days earlier.
- Chapter 8 says wine was purchased for seven consecutive days.
- It then says 短短的一个月, a short month.
- The next statement says 一周前的开窍大典, the ceremony a week earlier.
- Fang Zheng’s adjustment is then described as 最初的十几天下来, over the first ten-plus days.

These are not all introduced by the English translation. The repeatedly anchored week is stronger than the isolated month and ten-plus-day expressions in the immediate sequence. Still, chronology emendation should inspect adjoining chapters before locking the replacements.

A possible minimal coherence policy would retain the week and replace the incompatible month/ten-plus-day descriptions with “a single week” and “over these past few days”. That is a proposed harmonisation, not a newly discovered raw reading. The report was correct not to change these silently.

The Chapter 7 mention of reaching the fourth month is not automatically another error: a week can cross a calendar-month boundary.

### C. Source numerical discrepancy, not a proved single factual contradiction: casualties

Chapter 1’s antagonist accuses Fang Yuan of killing 千万人 to refine the Cicada. Chapter 2’s internal reflection gives 数十万人.

The number difference is real in the base and the Zhihu witness. However, the two speakers and rhetorical situations differ. Accusatory hyperbole, unreliable reporting, or a genuine authorial lapse are all possible. We cannot declare one motive or intended number proven.

Recommendation: retain the distinct numbers and perspectives. Do not insert “falsely accused” or harmonise them without stronger evidence. Also distinguish the English addition “innocents” from the source’s numerical claim; the quoted Chinese says people/lives, not explicitly innocents.

### D. Translation overgeneralisation still present: 44% as the C-grade limit

Live Chapter 6 `p2` says “This too was the limit of Grade C aptitude.” Read without qualification, that suggests 44% is the maximum for the whole grade. Chapter 91 explicitly allows up to 59%.

The Chinese early sentence is compressed and itself potentially misleading. A minimally clarifying rendering is:

> With his Grade C aptitude, this was as much as his aperture could hold.

That preserves Fang Yuan’s personal limit without redefining the grade. This was not resolved by merely adding spherical shape in C6-01.

### E. Source precision problem: height versus volume

Chapter 6’s wording links the sea’s height to 44%; Chapter 10 explicitly calls the entire sea’s volume 44% of aperture volume. For a spherical cavity these are not mathematically identical. The source may be using visual shorthand rather than a rigorous geometric specification.

Do not use the newly restored spherical shape as a licence to calculate and silently replace the source’s sea level. Log the distinction and describe capacity clearly where possible. It is a source imprecision to investigate, not grounds for a new numerical model.

### F. Battlefield compatibility

Still a valid continuity query, not a settled error. The earlier source allows exceptions; the Yama scene’s defensive formation is not expressly said to solve dao-mark interference. Baike does not settle this. Do not relocate the scene or invent a protective mechanism yet.

### G. Matters that should not currently be counted as confirmed novel contradictions

- The century-long bottleneck: the source supplies an aptitude-raising remedy.
- Bronze Skin Gu: revolution-dependent effects are explicitly distinguished.
- Jia Jin Sheng’s D-grade aptitude: no necessary contradiction is established.
- Xiong Jiao Man’s alleged second death: absent in the designated EPUB’s later scene.
- Central Continent’s huge extent: actually present in Chinese; a realism objection alone does not establish the intended smaller figure.
- Revolution 5 population: Earth-like density is an assumption, not a supplied census.

**Additional closure:** Volume 1, Chapter 24, `chapter32.html`, explicitly says the combat instructor is a Revolution 2 Gu Master: 他是二转蛊师. This resolves the instructor-cultivation-revolution query left open in my previous report. It should not be conflated with independently proving the revolution of every Gu he possesses.

## 8. Recommended next editorial decisions

1. Approve or reject the conjectural Chapter 22 numerical repair only after considering the whole-scene conflict.
2. Treat the fixes report as an editorial plan until revised HTML or a confirmed new public snapshot demonstrates implementation.
3. Adopt the sound meaning repairs, with the wording refinements above and no narrative em dashes.
4. Keep separate logs for source contradictions, translation defects, speculative harmonisations, and publication/version discrepancies.
5. Do not mark the meaning/completeness gate closed while the public text still lacks the P1 restorations and the C-grade personal-limit wording remains unresolved.

**Bottom line:** the new inconsistency is real, but the simplest-looking repair is not necessarily the least disruptive. The benchmark revisions mostly move in the right direction, but the attached report is not reliable evidence of what has actually been published.
