# Thought and viewpoint audit: corrected assessment

**The Omniarch | Chapters 1–8 | 13 October 2026**

## Correction notice

This document replaces my previous thought/viewpoint audit. I rechecked the Chinese passages behind my principal proposed changes. The earlier version overstated several stylistic preferences as corrections and supplied an over-broad rewrite of Chapter 3 that dropped source nuances. Do not implement its proposed replacements as a batch.

The old version is retained only as a superseded record in `editorial/thought-audit/Superseded-Thought-Audit.md`. No live-site changes have been made by me.

This pass audits my recommendations, not a fresh complete certification of every translated sentence. Public-wording references below refer to the saved live checks in this session; source judgements come from the supplied EPUB.

## 1. Chapter 6 p23: withdraw the pronoun-error finding

Chinese:

> 别人因此而失望，那就失望吧，又能如何呢？
> 别人的失望与我何干？重要的是自己心存希望！

Existing English:

> What is the disappointment of others to me? What matters is that one keeps hope in one’s own heart!

**Verdict: the existing pronoun movement is defensible.** The Chinese uses explicit 我 in the question and reflexive 自己 in the following statement. It also moves from narrated reflection into an unquoted first-person question. I should not have treated that movement itself as an English defect requiring regularisation.

自己 does not mechanically equal generic English “one” in all contexts. Here, both a character-specific reflexive interpretation and a maxim-like English formulation are possible. The point is that the existing “one keeps hope in one’s own heart” is defensible and does not require replacement with “I”.

Errors in my advice:

- “I **still** have hope” supplied a persistence emphasis not explicit in that clause.
- Replacing “one” with “I” narrowed the presentation while being offered as a correction.
- The heading “an unmarked first-person intrusion” made a source-supported turn sound like a defect.
- “The English should signal its entry” was too prescriptive. The source itself leaves the lines unquoted.

**Action:** retain the existing final clause. Italics or another thought cue are optional house-style decisions, not repairs demanded by the source. A separate idiomatic smoothing of the question, if desired, is “What does their disappointment have to do with me?” It does not require changing the following clause.

## 2. Chapter 3 p26–p27: retain a narrow clarity query, withdraw my wholesale rewrite

Chinese:

> 就算是亲弟弟又如何，没有亲情可言，只是个外人罢了，舍了也就舍了。
> 就算是沈翠长得再漂亮又如何，没有爱和忠心，不过是一具肉体。收入后宫？她还不配。
> 就算是舅父舅母，族长家老又如何，都是生命中的过客，何必费尽心机，耗散精力，来敲打这些路人？
> 呵呵。
> 只要不阻碍我赶路，那就一边玩自己的蛋去，踩都不屑踩。

The last sentence explicitly introduces 我. The preceding sentences often leave the personal reference implicit. A progression from reflection into direct first-person dismissal can therefore be source-supported. My rule that the entire passage should necessarily use one grammatical person was too rigid.

There remains a genuine English clarity concern in “So what if **he was his own younger brother**?” The two pronouns refer to different people. A narrow possible repair is “So what if Fang Zheng was his own younger brother?” This clarifies the referents without recasting the whole passage into direct thought.

My proposed full rewrite should not be used as a source-tight replacement:

| Source | My proposal | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| 亲弟弟 | my younger brother | Underplays the explicit blood/family relationship. |
| 舍了也就舍了 | I can let him go | Softens dismissive abandonment into permissive letting-go and introduces ability. |
| 长得再漂亮 | what if Shen Cui is beautiful | Drops the concessive force: however beautiful she might be. |
| 就算是舅父舅母…又如何 | As for my uncle and aunt… | Loses the repeated “even if / so what?” rhetorical structure. |
| 呵呵 | omitted | Drops the scoffing beat entirely. |

Some expressions were defensible paraphrases, but together they changed the texture of a passage I was supposedly repairing only for viewpoint. That exceeded the task.

**Action:** withdraw the full first-person model. Clarify specific English ambiguities without flattening source-supported shifts or dropping the rhetorical repetitions.

The Chinese title includes 请; the final body sentence does not. “Kindly” in the title and its absence from the body reflect those different source formulations, not a user-imposed restriction.

## 3. Chapter 8 p7a: downgrade the grammatical complaint; retain the source-order observation

Chinese:

> “唉，原本指望能得到花酒行者的酒虫，炼化它成为我的本命蛊。但是现在，寻找花酒行者尸骨仍旧未见端倪，也不知道什么时候能找到，或者被别人发现。保险起见，还是选一只月光蛊吧。”

The source presents a sigh, the original hope, the uncertainty, and then the precautionary decision as one quoted internal passage.

The saved English has:

> To be safe, I should choose a Moonlight Gu, Fang Yuan thought. He had hoped…

**“Fang Yuan thought” is already a clear tag.** Calling this a passage “without a clear boundary” was an overstatement. Moving from tagged direct thought to narrated explanation is grammatically legitimate.

There is nevertheless a real presentational change: the English places the decision before its reasons, whereas the source ends with the decision. Restoring that order would track the source more closely, but it is not the same issue as a wrong pronoun.

My alternative first-person version also left out the opening 唉. The following inward sigh may convey that effect, but I should have accounted for it explicitly rather than treating my excerpt as complete.

**Action:** no mandatory person-change repair. If revising for close source presentation, preserve the reasoning sequence and the sigh, while avoiding duplication with the following narration.

## 4. Chapter 8 p29: direct thought and free indirect thought are both legitimate treatments

Chinese:

> “这就是哥哥以前的感觉啊，真是美妙而又痛苦！”他不可避免地想到自己的哥哥古月方源，面对这样的议论和关注，哥哥他以前是怎么应对的呢？

The first sentence is quoted thought. The subsequent question is unquoted, attached to narration about Fang Zheng thinking of Fang Yuan.

- “So this is how **my brother** used to feel” is natural as direct thought.
- “So this was how **his brother** used to feel” is natural as free indirect thought.

The latter is a change of narrative presentation, not inherently a mistranslation or a case of Fang Zheng mistakenly referring to himself in the third person.

I was correct to identify Fang Zheng as the thinker. I was too categorical in treating a change to “my” as a resolved error rather than a decision to preserve direct presentation.

The subsequent “How had **his brother** dealt with…?” did not require conversion to quoted “How did **my brother** deal with…?” The conversion is optional, not demanded by the Chinese.

**Action:** the latest checked direct-thought wording can stand. Do not claim that the prior free-indirect wording was necessarily incorrect, or that both sentences must be quoted.

## 5. Chapter 8 p34/p40 and other quoted thoughts: formatting query only

Chinese includes:

> 自己是优秀的，这才是真相啊！

and:

> 自己这是怎么了？连和哥哥对视的勇气都没有么？
> 我已经变了，我已经彻底改变了！
> 这样想着…

These are Fang Zheng’s internal reflections. The source supports that identification. However, quotation marks in English do not by themselves prove that the translation has made him speak aloud. Context can establish quoted thought, just as it does elsewhere in the novel.

My recommendations for italics or “he told himself” are optional clarity devices. They are not omitted source words that must be restored.

The same downgrade applies to Chapter 1 p36, Chapter 4 p6/p28, Chapter 7 p26/p45, and the internal-to-spoken transition in Chapter 2 p33. Preserve their thought/speech distinction, but do not classify their shared quotation style alone as a meaning error.

Chinese:

> “二十四、二十五、二十六……二十七。”方源心中默默地数着…

The checked English has the count but omits its explicit silent/internal attribution. “Fang Yuan counted silently” is a close restoration, not an invented mental action.

The preceding aptitude-threshold passage is also quoted in the Chinese. There is no need to move it into narration merely because the final count needs its tag restored.

**Action:** restore the counting cue if aiming at completeness. Do not claim that the existing English necessarily makes him count aloud.

## 7. Chapter 2 p21: “your window” remains a supported idiomatic suggestion

Chinese, addressed to Fang Yuan:

> 看到哥哥的窗户没有关，就想悄悄的进来关了。

The checked English says “Seeing brother’s window wasn’t closed…”. “Your window” is natural English direct address, not a change in whose window it is.

A closer minimal alternative is:

> “I saw that your window wasn’t closed, so I wanted to slip in quietly and close it.”

This retains the negative construction rather than unnecessarily changing it to “was open”. The earlier “was open” was contextually reasonable, not a different window or speaker, but the minimal version is sufficient.

## Corrected editorial standard

- Start with what the source actually does, including shifts in person, register, repetition, and degree of explicitness.
- Do not regularise those shifts simply because uniform English would be smoother.
- Separate a genuine referent error from legitimate free indirect thought.
- Mark a paraphrase as a paraphrase, not a recovered source reading.
- When changing only viewpoint, do not simultaneously soften verbs, drop qualifiers, or reshape rhetorical structures.
- Quotation marks are not proof of audible speech, and italics are not proof of fidelity.
- Do not replace acceptable prose merely to implement a reviewer’s preference.

**Bottom line:** the previous audit overstated the extent of the problem. The source does not justify a blanket first-person or thought-formatting rewrite. The most solid remaining findings are the omitted silent-counting cue and specific English referent/idiom improvements, not the wholesale regularisation I proposed.
