# Bilingual QA — specimen sheet

**Reverend Insanity · The Omniarch translation** · chapters 1–9 (published as of 2026-10-12)
Comparator: the long-standing fan translation (fanmtl / novelfull numbering)
Source lines: Gu Zhen Ren 蛊真人, Volume 1

This is a **specimen**, not an audit: five fully-worked entries showing the format and the
standard of evidence, plus one forward-looking glossary item. A full run needs the Chinese
text of ch1–9 side by side with the English.

---

## How to read a row

| Flag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **FLATTEN** | an idiom or image present in the Chinese is absent or thinned in English |
| **INFLATE** | the English register is heavier than the source's |
| **ECHO** | accidental repetition inside a sentence or paragraph |
| **TERM** | glossary / consistency item — decide once, apply everywhere |
| **PRAISE** | rendering that beats the fan translation; keep it, and reuse the pattern |

**Key every row to the site's own paragraph id** (`<p id="pN">`). That makes feedback
actionable and unambiguous — "ch8 p21", not "somewhere in chapter 8".
(For reference, the 买醉 line is `p21` in ch8, not "P41".)

## ch8 p21 — "indulge in wine" · **FLATTEN + ECHO** · confidence ● firm

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chinese** | 哥哥，你果真又来酒馆买醉！跟我来，舅父舅母要见你。 |
| **Omniarch** | "Brother, you have truly returned to the wine shop to indulge in wine once more! Come with me; our uncle and aunt wish to see you." |
| **Fan (ch8)** | "Big brother, you really did go to the tavern and buy alcohol! Follow me, aunt and uncle want to see you." |
| **Issue** | 买醉 is not "drinking wine" — it is *buying drunkenness*: deliberate, goal-directed drinking. "Indulge in wine" drops 醉 entirely, which is the one element every dictionary treats as the core (汉典: 沽酒而饮; 国语辞典: 買酒喝，比喻行樂或解愁). Secondary: "wine shop … wine" echoes twice in one sentence. Tertiary: 果真 = "sure enough / as I suspected", not "truly". |
| **Suggested** | "Brother, you've really come back to the wine shop to **drink yourself senseless** again! Come with me — our uncle and aunt want to see you." |
| **Discussion** | "drown your sorrows" imports a motive: 借酒浇愁 is the *specific* sorrow idiom and the author declined to use it — a fair objection. But the same strictness indicts "indulge," which keeps neither the motive nor the drunkenness. Ranked: *drink yourself senseless* > *drink yourself into a stupor* > *drown your sorrows* > *indulge in wine*. Register note: the first is blunt — which suits an angry fifteen-year-old. |
| **Status** | open · author's call |

## ch8 p24 — "things remained while people have changed" · **PRAISE** · confidence ● firm

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chinese** | 短短的一个月，却已物是人非了。 |
| **Omniarch** | "A mere month, yet things remained while people have changed." |
| **Fan (ch8)** | "Just a short period of one month has gone by, yet humans change." |
| **Why it wins** | 物是人非 is a four-character idiom whose whole weight is the *contrast*: the things (物) are still there, the people (人) are not. The fan version keeps only the second half and loses the melancholy entirely; the Omniarch restores both halves — and promotes it to the chapter title. Model for the rest of the project. |

## ch8 p23 — the wind and the hair · **PRAISE** · confidence ● firm

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chinese** | 一阵风呼啸地吹来，拂起哥哥散乱的黑发，吹起弟弟的衣摆。 |
| **Omniarch** | "A gust of wind howled past, lifting the elder brother's dishevelled black hair and fluttering the younger brother's robes." |
| **Fan (ch8)** | "A gust of wind blew, lifting up the older brother's messy short hair, the lower hem of the younger brother's robes swishing around." |
| **Why it wins** | The source says 黑发 — *black* hair. The fan translator invented "short", which is not in the text. The Omniarch keeps the colour, upgrades 吹 to "howled" (matching 呼啸), and preserves the balanced two-clause structure instead of flattening it into one lopsided sentence. |

## ch8 p36 — "breathed a fierce sigh of relief" · **PRAISE** · confidence ● firm

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chinese** | 因此，看到方源拎着酒坛，头发散乱，衣衫不整的模样，古月方正心中狠狠地舒了一口气，呼吸莫名地轻松了许多。 |
| **Omniarch** | "Therefore, seeing Fang Yuan with wine jars in hand, his hair dishevelled and clothes untidy, Gu Yue Fang Zheng breathed a fierce sigh of relief, his breathing inexplicably eased." |
| **Fan (ch8)** | "Hence when he saw Fang Yuan holding the wine jars, his hair messy and clothes untidy, Gu Yue Fang Zheng felt relieved, his breathing also becoming a lot easier." |
| **Why it wins** | 狠狠地 — *fiercely*, a verb hard on the sigh — survives. This is the important one for calibrating criticism: the translator is not simply ornate. Where the source is violent or precise, he is violent or precise. Flag inflation only where the Chinese is plain. |

## ch8 p30 — "resonated from behind him" · **INFLATE** · confidence ● firm

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chinese** | 就在这时，身后响起古月方正的声音： |
| **Omniarch** | "Just then, Gu Yue Fang Zheng's voice resonated from behind him: …" |
| **Issue** | 响起 is "sounded / rang out". "Resonated" adds reverberation and depth that the source does not have — a small tax on every plain verb of speech, and the clearest example of the house style's one systematic flaw. |
| **Suggested** | "Just then, Gu Yue Fang Zheng's voice came from behind him:" |

## FORWARD-LOOKING — glossary item · **TERM** · confidence ●◐ likely

The wine place and the inn are **not the same building**, and English is about to blur them.

| Chinese | Where | Render as | Why now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 酒肆 / 酒馆 | the wine counter with a drinking hall (ch8) | "wine shop" — keep as-is | Already used consistently; keeps 酒 visible, which matters: the Liquor Worm, the Flower-Wine Monk, the jars. |
| 客栈 | the village's only inn — first appears around ch10–11 | "inn" | **Do not** reuse "shop." A 客栈 has rooms upstairs and a dining hall below; a shopkeeper owns neither. |
| 掌柜 (of the 客栈) | same | "innkeeper" | 掌柜 is one Chinese word for any proprietor — shop, inn, or household. English needs the split, or the reader will picture the wine shop again. |
| 宿舍 | clan lodgings (ch9 p68) | "lodgings" — already good | Avoid "dormitory" (institutional) and "hostel" (the fan translation's drift). |
| 房东 | the bamboo-house landlord (later) | "landlord" | 

The fan translation drifts across all of these — *tavern* (ch8), *wine tavern* (ch110), *inn*,
*hostel*, *apartment*, *restaurant hall*. That drift, plus one Chinese word for every
proprietor, is why readers conflate the wine shop, the inn, and the rented bamboo house into
"the place he lived." Fixing the glossary here prevents the confusion at the source.
