---

Due to widely known reasons, this book was not only subjected to censorship before its completion, but several pivotal chapters critical to character development were also purged or heavily altered. The author was unavoidably bound hand and foot during serialization. 

After *Reverend Insanity* was targeted by internet clean-up campaigns, readers and fans took it upon themselves to preserve and restore the work. This editor originally intended to add back those scenes that could never pass official censorship—highlighting Fang Yuan's demonic nature even more sharply without altering the plot. However, out of respect for the original text, no unauthorized modifications were introduced here. In the future, a separate project may be launched to explore the unrestrained narrative that the author was never permitted to write. As for why the author had long been bound in shackles, reading the notes below will make it abundantly clear.

Although the novel remains unfinished in official publication, fortunately, the vast majority of plot arcs have already concluded, leaving perhaps only around fifty chapters for a final resolution (which, if pressed, could have been wrapped up within ten). Perhaps this is just as well: had it continued to the very end, it might have merely concluded with achieving eternal life or similar tropes. Concluding as it did due to an "irresistible force" of censorship keeps it untainted by cliché, setting it far apart from the common herd in an unconventional way.

Based on the meticulously corrected EPUB edition compiled by netizen scholars, this release restores the previously censored passages back to their original drafts, providing context and textual comparisons.

Because the majority of this labor was completed by fellow netizens, this secondary editor will remain anonymous.

### **Preserved Author's Notes and Editor's Annotations in this Edition**

#### **Volume 1, Chapter 151: Demonic Nature**
* **Author's Original Note:**  
  *"I originally wanted to write a literal, true-to-its-name 'Man-Beast Life Burial Gu' [人兽葬生蛊]. But considering the censorship board [the river crab], I only wrote 'Life Burial.' As for the preceding part, fellow daoists of the demonic path are invited to fill in the blanks with their own imagination."*
* **Editor's Note:**  
  *Based on the author's note, the literal implication of "Man-Beast" is unmistakable. Beyond that, my personal interpretation is that "Burial-Birth" also implied rapid conception—that under the Gu's influence, Yao Le was intended to rapidly bear the child of beast and man, serving as the bear-mother of a new beastman species before all were sacrificed to refine the Gu. Fang Yuan as the creator-god of a new beast race: truly the "greatest philanthropist of the Five Regions." The censored version deleted the scene of Yao Le being consumed by the bear, stripping away the tension and reducing the scene to a hasty, anticlimactic afterthought, failing to showcase Fang Yuan's chilling cruelty.*

#### **Volume 2, Chapter 27: Flesh and Bone Reunion**
* **Editor's Note:**  
  *This chapter was subsequently altered due to censorship. We have selected the original version here, which far better illuminates Fang Yuan's monstrous nature and his unyielding ethos of employing any means to achieve his goals. In the censored version, this scene lost all its impact: a defining chapter highlighting Fang Yuan's demonic resolve was turned into a generic dungeon raid and map change. The censored version simply wrote that Bai Hua and Bai Sheng, as descendants of Grey Bone Scholar, were teleported away in flames to meet another destiny. However, the author never followed up on that alteration later in the story; if they had survived, the twins should have reappeared to oppose Fang Yuan, which they never did. This proves the author was merely forced to alter the scene under external pressure.*

#### **Volume 2, Chapter 28: Footless Bird, Spread Your Wings and Soar!**
* **Author's Original Note:**  
  *"What is the demonic path? A 'dao' must possess its own underlying thought and philosophy. Everyone has their own interpretation of the demonic path, but in my eyes, the footless bird undoubtedly embodies the demonic spirit. The demonic path is ultimately cruel—not only toward others, but toward oneself. Hence, it is naturally unloved by the world."*

### **A Personal Take (一家之言)**
The biggest controversy surrounding this novel has always been: *Is it really necessary to write something so dark?* 

Unfortunately, reality itself is just that dark. Reality has always devoured human flesh—why should one not write it? Reality exceeds fiction by far. What is regrettable is that while the author touched upon the underlying backdrop, he remained constrained by a focus on organizational and political apparatuses. What creates all of this is not merely an institutional system, but the foundational socioeconomic productive relations beneath it. The author read related theoretical works, yet did not fully transcend the bourgeois ideological horizon. 

Nonetheless, the ultimate depiction of extreme self-interest in the novel serves as one of the sharpest satires of capitalist logic. I recommend readers explore the foundational works of Marx, Lenin, and Mao Zedong for deeper clarity.

## 3. The Author's Preface: Either March Towards Success, or March Towards Destruction
*(Translated from `OEBPS/Text/chapter2.html`)*

### **Preface: Either March Towards Success, or March Towards Destruction**
*By Gu Zhen Ren (蛊真人)*

Over the past few months, I have endured the pain life has bestowed upon me, struggling both physically and mentally.

Life is like a balance scale: one pan holds "reality," and the other holds "the inner heart." For me, the pan of "reality" grew heavier and heavier, causing the scale to tilt, and from that imbalance arose pain.

People often walk for so long that they become lost in the scenery by the roadside, forgetting why they set out in the first place.

That was my state: amidst the pain, I felt waves of bewilderment.

From the completion of my previous book until now, during this period of rest, I have been striving to find my direction—searching for the original reason why I embarked on this journey.

It was roughly five or six years ago. Back then, I had been reading web novels for some time.

*Journey to the Ethereal* (飘渺之旅) was the first, followed by *Blasphemy* (亵渎), *Zhu Xian* (诛仙), *Mad God* (狂神), *Beastmaster* (驭兽斋), *Beast Blood Boiling* (兽血沸腾), *Shushan* (蜀山), *Only I Am Immortal* (惟我独仙), and many others.

I read countless novels, yet a knot of frustration gradually accumulated in my chest. In almost every book, the protagonists were bathed in heaven-defying fortune, upright, open, and morally exemplary. Meanwhile, the villains were mostly foolish, insane, or grotesque; possessing resounding titles, they were nothing more than paper tigers—glamorous on the outside, but wilting the second they encountered the protagonist.

And so, a desire formed within me: I wanted to see a true, genuine Great Villain.

This villain would be an eccentric nonconformist: aloof, dark, decisive in slaughter, and never faltering. He would never disguise his evil, and he would harbor zero hypocritical virtue.

His appearance alone would radiate menace—demonic aura surging, murderous intent permeating the air. His greatest specialty would be trampling rules underfoot and slaughtering the prestigious righteous factions.

Alone, he would stand as an enemy to the entire world, sowing dread across all living beings. He would be a Grandmaster of the Evil Path: at times overbearing and domineering, at times insidious and cunning, at times icy and ruthless. Standing atop the peak, sneering down at the world, every "hero" who dared challenge him would meet a miserable end.

His methods would be cruel; perhaps he would manipulate Gu insects—in short, someone who was unmistakably dangerous.

So what should his name be? ... *Super Demon King? Old Black Mountain Demon? Green-Robed Ancestor?*

No, no, no.

After pondering, I thought: why not call him **Gu Zhen Ren**?

It just so happened that I needed to register an account on Qidian at the time. OK, and thus this pen name was born.

In television dramas, we often see this trope: the arch-villain defeats the hero, needing only one final lethal blow. But instead, the villain begins to chatter and gloat endlessly. The hero secretly gathers his strength, makes one desperate final gamble, turns the tables, and wins a total victory.

And if the villain finds himself at a disadvantage, the scene goes like this: the hero unleashes his power, striking the villain down into the dirt. Faced with the hero’s blade, the villain suddenly weeps bitterly, swearing he will turn over a new leaf. The hero hesitates out of "benevolence and mercy." At that moment, the villain launches a sneak attack—and fails. The hero, "perceptive as a hawk," suppresses the villain completely, hardens his heart, roars *"Despicable scoundrel!"*, and finally executes him.

Then there are those villains who kidnap the hero’s friends and family, yet almost never succeed in their extortion. Even with a massive advantage, they inexplicably collapse due to all manner of *force majeure*.

Six years ago, when I created the pen name "Gu Zhen Ren," I held a singular wish: to witness a magnificent, evil antagonist. 

He would possess his own code of conduct. Shunned by the world, roaming free and unconstrained, he would disdain offering any explanation. He would be decisive in slaughter, never trapped by romance, and his knees would never go weak in the presence of a beauty.

His renowned name would awe the four seas, striking terror into the hearts of men.

Those who understood him would admire, revere, and respect him; those who hated him would grind their teeth in fury, employing every scheme imaginable, only to watch him grow stronger and stronger.

He would walk his own lonely road, wrapped in solitude, his indifferent eyes seeing through every facade and falsehood.

He would sneer at criticism and condemnation. He would meet flattery with silent mockery.

He would endure loneliness—nay, he would relish loneliness.

**He would either march towards success, or march towards destruction.**

He is a warrior, embarking on a solitary pilgrimage through the darkness, cleaving through brambles and thorns. Nothing can bar his way: *if a god stands in the path, kill the god; if a Buddha stands in the path, slay the Buddha!*

This dream remained deeply buried in my heart. Over the years of writing, I contemplated putting pen to paper several times, but ultimately hesitated. I felt my skill was not yet sufficient.

Years slipped by, and I experienced many tribulations. Compared to when I first started writing, the world and people around me have completely changed.

The past is somewhat painful to look back upon, and this dream was buried beneath the dust of time, gradually forgotten. Even when I occasionally recalled it, I felt underprepared, lacking the mastery to tell such an exhilarating story.

Yet over these past few days, as I halted my relentless march forward and looked back, an epiphany struck me: **Life is not like cooking a dish.**

When cooking, you prepare the oil, salt, soy sauce, and vinegar in advance before firing up the wok. 

Life does not wait for preparation. Even if "everything is ready," aren't you still waiting for that favorable east wind?

Life is always hurried. Most things happen abruptly, with little to no preparation at all. Fate is fickle and unpredictable; no one can foresee what tomorrow brings.

And so, I made my decision: against the backdrop of the prophesied 2012 apocalypse, I would write a new novel.

This book shall be titled **《Reverend Insanity》 (蛊真人)**.

No title could be more fitting.

This is the fruit born of the seed planted six years ago.

Using my pen name as the book title carries no hidden arrogance; it is purely an expression of fulfilling a dream. I want to realize that long-held aspiration and reclaim that bygone passion.

Therefore, this book will be vastly different from standard novels, and distinctly different from my previous works.

Because the protagonist of this book is an arch-villain: utterly ruthless, stopping at nothing to achieve his goals, uncompromisingly evil! And the "antagonists" in this story will mostly be upright young heroes, peerless prodigies, or honorable righteous sects.

In this story, you will see such scenes: when the protagonist defeats a righteous hero, he will never chatter; a single blade will snuff out any possibility of a comeback. He will kidnap the hero’s family and friends to force them into submission.

He will never show mercy. Regardless of who the opponent is, as long as they are an enemy—no matter how powerful, he will fight; no matter how breathtakingly beautiful she is, he will cut her down without hesitation.

Yes, this book will kill female characters—specifically stunning beauties, slaughtered without remorse.

Those who expect a protagonist whose knees go weak at the sight of a pretty face, whose eyes turn into hearts, or who constantly dreams of romantic escapades will find none of that here.

I know this will disappoint many readers, but please understand: could someone who abandons his principles the moment he sees a pretty woman ever possess the demeanor of a peerless demon overlord?

That is merely being lovesick.

In this novel, I want to depict the madness of an evil boss, his rebellion, his ruthlessness, his unbridled freedom, and his audacious will.

Without question, this book will spark controversy. In the eyes of forum critics, this is termed "having perverse morals" (三观不正).

Yes, I openly admit it here: this is indeed a book with non-traditional morals.

Reading is meant for pleasure and entertainment.

This book is very dark; read with caution.

Those who wish to read are wholeheartedly welcomed.

Those who cannot stomach it, who feel unhappy, or who feel their worldview, values, and morality are being offended—I respectfully see you off!

After all, reading novels is about finding joy. If it brings you no happiness, put it down and pick another book. Qidian has countless wonderful works, and I will recommend some along the way.

The above is both the preface and a disclaimer for this book.

## 4. Volume 1, Chapter 1: Even in Death, the Demonic Heart Does Not Regret
*(Translated from `OEBPS/Text/chapter9.html`)*

### **Volume 1: Nature Does Not Change**
### **Chapter 1: Even in Death, the Demonic Heart Does Not Regret**

"Fang Yuan, hand over the Spring Autumn Cicada obediently, and I will grant you a swift death!"

"Old Demon Fang, do not even dream of resisting! Today, all the major sects of our righteous path have joined forces to raze your demonic lair to the ground. An inescapable heaven-and-earth net has long been laid here—today, your head and body will surely be severed!"

"Fang Yuan, you wretched demon! To refine the Spring Autumn Cicada, you slaughtered tens of millions of innocent lives! You have committed monstrous atrocities, unpardonable and beyond reckoning!"

"Demon, three hundred years ago you violated me, took my purity, slaughtered my entire family, and exterminated all nine branches of my clan! From that very moment, I have wished for nothing more than to feast upon your flesh and drink your blood! Today, I shall make you wish you were dead!!"

……

Clad in a tattered emerald-green robe, his hair disheveled and his body drenched in blood, Fang Yuan surveyed his surroundings.

The mountain wind whipped his blood-soaked robe, fluttering like a battle flag with crisp, sharp snaps.

Crimson blood surged outward from hundreds of wounds across his body. Standing there for merely a moment, a massive pool of blood had already gathered beneath his feet.

Surrounded on all sides by enemies, every avenue of escape had long been cut off.

The outcome was decided; today, death was certain.

Fang Yuan saw through the situation with complete clarity. Yet even with death looming directly over him, his expression remained unchanged, impassive and calm.

His gaze was deep and serene, like an ancient well or a secluded pool—bottomless, as it had always been.

The righteous heroes besieging him were either venerable elders of prominent sects or celebrated young prodigies famous across the land. Right now, encircling Fang Yuan tightly, some roared in fury, some sneered coldly, some narrowed their eyes with guarded vigilance, while others clutched their wounds, looking upon him with lingering dread.

None dared make a move; all feared Fang Yuan's dying counterattack.

In this manner, the tense standoff dragged on for three full *shichen* [six hours]. The sun sank toward the west, its dying rays igniting the clouds along the mountain rim, blazing across the sky like a brilliant fire.

Fang Yuan, who had stood motionless as a statue throughout, slowly turned around.

A sudden stir rippled through the crowd; the assembled heroes retreated a large step in unison.

By now, the greyish-white rocks beneath Fang Yuan's feet had been dyed dark crimson with his blood. His face, pale from massive blood loss, was bathed in the evening glow, suddenly taking on an enchanting, radiant luster.

Gazing upon the verdant mountains and the setting sun, Fang Yuan chuckled softly: 

*"Green mountains beneath the setting sun, autumn moon and spring breeze.*  
*Truly, in the morning one's hair is like black silk, by dusk it turns to snow;*  
*Right and wrong, success and failure, all vanish in the blink of an eye."*

As he spoke these words, scenes from his past life on Earth suddenly surfaced before his eyes.

He had originally been a Chinese student on Earth who, by a twist of fate, transmigrated to this world. Tossing and turning through three hundred years of hardship, then roaming and dominating the world for more than two hundred—five hundred years of flowing time had slipped away in the blink of an eye.

Countless memories deeply buried in his heart sprang to life at this instant, replaying vividly before him.

*"In the end, I failed after all,"* Fang Yuan sighed inwardly, feeling a touch of wistfulness, yet not a shred of regret.

He had long anticipated such an outcome. When he made his choice all those centuries ago, he was already mentally prepared for this day.

The so-called Demonic Path was to cultivate no virtuous karma, to kill and burn, tolerated by neither heaven nor earth, making enemies of the entire world, yet still rampaging and roaming freely according to one's desires.

*"If the newly refined Spring Autumn Cicada truly works, then in my next life, I shall still be a demon!"* 

With this thought, Fang Yuan could not help but burst into hearty laughter.

"Old Demon, what are you laughing at?!"

"Everyone watch out! The demon is at death's door and prepares to strike back!"

"Hand over the Spring Autumn Cicada at once!!"

The crowd pressed forward aggressively. At that very instant—**BOOM!**

Fang Yuan decisively detonated himself.

……

The spring rain fell in fine, steady drizzles, silently nourishing Mount Qing Mao.

The night was deep; a cool breeze whispered through the gentle mist.

Yet Mount Qing Mao was far from dark. From the mountainside down to the foothills, faint, shimmering lights glowed, as if draping a brilliant ribbon of radiance across the slope.

These lights came from rows of stilt houses—stretching not to tens of thousands, but easily numbering in the thousands.

This was Gu Yue Village, nestled upon Mount Qing Mao, lending a vibrant touch of human life to the vast and tranquil mountain range.

At the very center of Gu Yue Village stood a grand and magnificent pavilion. An ancestral ceremony was currently underway, illuminating the pavilion with dazzling brilliance and festive splendor.

"May our ancestors bless and protect us! May many talented youths emerge in this Awakening Ceremony to bring fresh blood and hope to our clan!" 

The Gu Yue Clan Leader appeared middle-aged, his temples touched with frost. Dressed in solemn, plain white ceremonial robes, he knelt erect upon the brownish-yellow floorboards, his palms pressed together and his eyes shut tight in earnest prayer.

He faced a tall, black-lacquered altar with three tiers, enshrining the spirit tablets of the ancestors. On either side of the tablets stood red-copper incense burners, thin plumes of fragrant smoke coiling upward into the air.

Behind him knelt over a dozen figures dressed in matching white robes. These were the clan elders and decision-makers, each wielding power over various affairs of the clan.

After concluding his prayers, the Clan Leader was the first to bend forward, palms flat against the wooden floorboards, kowtowing. His forehead met the brown floor with soft, rhythmic thuds.

The elders behind him, their expressions solemn, followed suit in silence.

For a time, the ancestral hall was filled only with the gentle sounds of foreheads knocking against the floor.

When the ritual concluded, everyone slowly rose from the floor and quietly filed out of the solemn hall.

In the corridor, the elders breathed a quiet sigh of relief, the tense atmosphere easing noticeably.

Murmurs of conversation soon began to rise:

"Time truly flies. In the blink of an eye, another full year has gone by."

"The previous Awakening Ceremony feels like it happened just yesterday; the memory is still vivid."

"Tomorrow is the annual Awakening Ceremony. I wonder what kind of fresh blood our clan will produce this year?"

"Sigh, I only hope a youth with Grade A aptitude emerges. It has been three years since our Gu Yue clan had such a genius."

"Indeed. Both Bai Village and Xiong Village have seen remarkable talents emerge over recent years. In particular, Bai Ning Bing of the Bai clan—his natural talent is truly terrifying."

Whoever mentioned the name Bai Ning Bing, a shadow of worry involuntarily crossed the faces of the clan elders.

This youth's talent was extraordinary. In just two short years, he had already cultivated to Rank 3 Gu Master, standing unrivaled among the younger generation. Even the older generation felt immense pressure from this rising star.

Given time, he would undoubtedly become the pillar of Bai Village, or at the very least a powerhouse capable of dominating his own domain. No one ever doubted this.

"However, among the youths participating in this year's Awakening Ceremony, it is not as though we have no hope."

"Quite right. A genius youth has appeared in the Fang lineage. He could speak at three months and walk at four. By age five, he could compose poems and recite verses—exceptionally intelligent and overflowing with talent. It is just a pity his parents died early, and he is currently being raised by his aunt and uncle."

"Mm, he possesses precocious wisdom and grand ambitions. In recent years, I have heard of the poems he composed—*Bring in the Wine*, *Ode to the Plum Blossom*, and *River Town*. He is truly a genius!"

The Gu Yue Clan Leader was the last to exit the ancestral hall. As he slowly closed the door, the murmurs of the elders reached his ears.

He immediately knew they were discussing a youth named Gu Yue Fang Yuan.

As the leader of the clan, he naturally paid close attention to outstanding juniors, and Gu Yue Fang Yuan was the most prominent and dazzling among them.

Experience had shown that those gifted with photographic memories or strength rivaling an adult from childhood almost always possessed superior cultivation talent.

*"If this youth tests with Grade A aptitude and is properly nurtured, he may well be able to contend with Bai Ning Bing. Even if he only has Grade B aptitude, he will certainly become a stalwart pillar and a banner for the Gu Yue clan in the future. Yet with such precocious intellect, Grade B is unlikely—it is overwhelmingly probable that he has Grade A."*

With this thought, the corners of the Clan Leader's lips lifted slightly into a smile.

He cleared his throat with a dry cough and addressed the elders: "Everyone, the hour is late. For the sake of tomorrow's Awakening Ceremony, please make sure to rest well tonight and conserve your energy."

Hearing this, the elders paused slightly. In the glances they exchanged, a subtle trace of vigilance was concealed.

The Clan Leader's words were polite and restrained, but everyone understood their deeper meaning.

Every year, in order to recruit talented juniors into their own factions, the elders fought tooth and nail until their faces flushed red and heads rolled.

It was indeed time to rest and gather strength, so they could contend fiercely tomorrow.

Especially for that Gu Yue Fang Yuan—the probability of him possessing Grade A aptitude was exceptionally high. Moreover, both his parents were deceased, leaving him and his brother as the only surviving orphans of the Fang lineage. If one could bring him into their own faction and groom him well, it could guarantee a century of prosperity for their line!

"However, let me make the ugly words clear first. Compete openly and honorably; underhanded schemes that harm clan unity will not be tolerated. Elders, bear this firmly in mind!" the Clan Leader warned sternly.

"We wouldn't dare, we wouldn't dare."

"We shall keep it firmly in mind."

"Then we shall take our leave. Please do not trouble yourself to see us off, Clan Leader."

Harboring their own calculations, the elders dispersed one by one.

Before long, the long corridor grew quiet and desolate. Slanting spring rain and a cool draft drifted through the open window; the Clan Leader stepped lightly toward the window frame.

Instantly, the crisp, moist mountain air filled his senses, refreshing to the soul.

From the third floor of this pavilion, the Clan Leader could take in most of Gu Yue Village in a single glance.

At this late hour of the night, the majority of homes in the village still burned with lamplight—quite unlike ordinary days.

Tomorrow was the Awakening Ceremony, touching upon everyone's vital interests. A mixture of excitement and tension enveloped the clansmen's hearts, and naturally many found it difficult to sleep peacefully.

"This is the future hope of our clan," the Clan Leader sighed softly, the flickering lights reflected in his eyes.

……

At this very moment, another pair of clear eyes quietly gazed upon those twinkling lights in the deep of the night, brimming with complex emotions.

"Gu Yue Village... this is five hundred years ago?! The Spring Autumn Cicada truly worked..." 

Fang Yuan's gaze was dark and profound as he stood beside the window, letting the wind and rain beat against his body.

The effect of the Spring Autumn Cicada was to reverse time. Ranked seventh among the Ten Great Mystical Gu, it was naturally no ordinary existence.

In short: rebirth.

"Reborn using the Spring Autumn Cicada... back to five hundred years ago!" 

Fang Yuan reached out his hand, staring intently at his young, tender, and somewhat pale palm. Then he slowly clenched it into a fist, feeling the vivid reality with all his strength.

Beside his ears, the gentle pitter-patter of drizzle tapped against the window lattice. 

He closed his eyes slowly, opening them only after a long moment, letting out a wistful sigh: 

"Five hundred years of experience... it truly feels like a dream."

Yet he knew with absolute certainty: this was no dream.
