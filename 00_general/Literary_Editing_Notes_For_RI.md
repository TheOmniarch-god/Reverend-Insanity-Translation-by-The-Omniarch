## 1. The Elements of Eloquence (Mark Forsyth) — Rhetorical Power

**Core idea:** Sentences sound epic because of hidden structures, not big words.

**Tools for RI:**

- **Apposition (renaming):** "Moonlight Gu, the guardian Gu worm of the Gu Yue clan," — second phrase renames first, adds lore without new sentence. Use for Gu introductions.
- **Isocolon / Tricolon (balanced length):** "All vanish in a turn of the head." — 3 beats, snap. Avoid "in the turning of a head" (gerund breaks beat).
- **Chiasmus (ABBA):** "Strength requires rest, rest requires wisdom" — useful for Ren Zu fables.
- **Anadiplosis (end→start):** "He gave his youth for strength. Strength gave him food." — creates fable chain for Ren Zu.
- **Application to erudite:** One erudite word per image is eloquence. "verdant massif" = one erudite (verdant) + one noun. "luxuriant verdant massif" = two adjectives same meaning = breaks isocolon, becomes purple.

**Rule:** If you use an erudite word (redolence, clandestine, chartreuse, plumaged, verdant), let it stand alone. Don't pair with synonym. That's stacking = purple. Balanced = one showcase per clause.

## 2. Steering the Craft (Ursula K. Le Guin) — Sentence Architecture

**Core idea:** Sentence length = psychological effect. Crowding vs leaping.

**Tools for RI:**

- **Crowding:** Packing too many modifiers into one noun. BAD: "a sprawling, chartreuse expanse" (sprawling + chartreuse + expanse = 3 for 黄绿). GOOD: "a pale wash of chartreuse" (one colour, one texture).
- **Leaping:** Short sentence after long for punch. For Ren Zu: "All his teeth had fallen out. He could not even chew wild fruit." — dry, two short leaps = ache.
- **Viewpoint:** Fang Yuan's POV is cold, detached. Short sentences for his thoughts, longer for clan elders' pompous speech.
- **Rhythm for lore:** Lore facts (present tense) should be steady, medium length. Narrative action past tense can be short/long varied.

**Exercise applied to Line 19:**
- BAD (crowded): "In the proximity lay a bamboo forest." (stilted) + "exceptionally sharp, just like a spearhead" (exceptionally + just like = hedging)
- GOOD (leapt): "Close by lay a bamboo grove." + "each stalk straight as a line, its tip sharp as a spearhead." — one image per clause.

**Rule:** After a long descriptive sentence, follow with short for breath. Ch7 woods paragraph: long "Tender shoots had just opened, a pale wash of chartreuse, where plumaged finches perched..." + short "A spring wind blew in..."

## 3. Several Short Sentences About Writing (Verlyn Klinkenborg) — Stripping Filler

**Core idea:** Every syllable must earn its place. Remove syntactic filler.

**Tools for RI:**

- **Filler words to hunt:** "very", "extremely", "slightly", "already", "actually", "just", "quite", "rather". Example: "each one as straight as a line, its tip exceptionally sharp, just like a spearhead" → "each stalk straight as a line, its tip sharp as a spearhead." — dropped "one", "exceptionally", "just like".
- **Measure words:** Keep when literal and beautiful: 一块 = a piece (keep), 一股 = a wisp (keep). Don't drop for smoothness if it's accurate.
- **"Base of the plate" vs "base":** 底盘 = base plate. "Base of the plate" adds "of the plate" filler. Strip to "silver base".
- **Noun + of + noun chains:** Not always filler — often shows origin/possession. "Scent of green hills" is good (scent *from* hills). BAD chain is redundant: "the base of the plate of the silver base" or "the fresh air of verdant mountains and clear waters and scattering it across the world" — too many "of/and". Keep precise "of" that shows source, strip only redundant ones like "base of the plate" → "silver base" (底盘 itself is base, not base *of* plate).

**Test:** Read sentence aloud. If you can remove a word and meaning stays, remove it. If removal loses accuracy (wisp, piece, of clansmen), keep it.
