# Reverend Insanity — Author's Preface

## Either Toward Success or Toward Destruction

In the past few months, I have lived through a kind of pain that life bestows, and both body and mind have struggled within it.

Life is like a balance scale. On one end you place “reality,” on the other “the inner self.” For me, the side holding reality grew heavier and heavier, until the scale lost its balance, and from that came pain.

People often walk a road for so long that they lose themselves in the scenery along the way, and forget why they set out in the first place.

That is what happened to me. In pain, I felt waves of bewilderment.

In the rest since my last book was completed until now, I have been trying to find my direction, to recover the reason I first set out.

It was five or six years ago. By then I had already been reading novels for some time. The Ethereal Journey was the first, then Blasphemy, Zhu Xian, Mad God, The Beast-Taming Studio, Boiling Beast Blood, Mount Shu, I Alone Am Immortal, and so on.

I read a great many novels, but a frustration built up inside me. Most protagonists I met enjoyed extraordinary fortune and were upright and virtuous. Most villains, meanwhile, were foolish, crazed, and ugly. Their reputations were impressive, but they were paper tigers, imposing until they met the protagonist, when they wilted.

So I began to think: I wanted to see a genuine archvillain.

This villain would go his own way, proud, solitary, and dark, decisive in killing and never weak at the knees. He would never conceal his own evil, and there would be no pretense of goodness.

At a glance his image would be unmistakably evil, his demonic aura stark, his killing intent suffusing the air. What he did best would be trampling rules and slaughtering the famed powers of the Righteous Path.

Alone, he would stand against the entire world, spreading terror among all living things. He would be a great master of the Demonic Path, sometimes arrogant and overbearing, sometimes sinister and cunning, sometimes cold and merciless. He would stand proudly at the summit, scorning the world, and every brave soul who challenged him would come to a miserable end.

His methods would be cruel. Perhaps he would even toy with Gu. In short, one look would tell you he was no good.

Then what would his name be… Super Demon King? Old Black Mountain Demon? Green-Robed Ancestor?

No, no, no.

After thinking it over, I decided: why not call him Gu Zhen Ren?

As it happened, I needed to create an account on Qidian at the time. Okay, and so that username was born.

Some television dramas often play out like this: the villain defeats the protagonist and needs only one final blow to kill him. Then the villain starts rambling. Meanwhile, the protagonist secretly gathers strength, makes one last desperate effort, and invariably turns the tables to win a complete victory.

If the villain is the one at a disadvantage, the scene goes like this instead: after the protagonist unleashes his power, the defeated villain lies sprawled on the ground. Facing the protagonist’s sword, he suddenly bursts into tears and vows to mend his ways. The ’kindhearted’ protagonist hesitates. The villain seizes the moment to counterattack, and fails. The protagonist, ’seeing through everything with perfect clarity,’ thoroughly subdues him. His heart hardens, he shouts, ’Despicable, shameless scum!’ and finally kills the villain.

Then there are villains who kidnap the protagonist’s friends and family but almost never succeed in using them as leverage. Even with an enormous advantage, they somehow lose to the protagonist through all manner of circumstances beyond their control.

It was about six years ago that I created the ID Gu Zhen Ren, with a wish in my heart: to witness an evil archvillain. He would have his own way of doing things. Rejected by the world, he would act freely and without restraint, disdaining explanation. He would be decisive in killing, never entangled by affection, and never weak at the knees before a beautiful woman.

His fearsome reputation would make the four seas tremble, people would pale at the mention of him.

Those who understood him would admire him, revere him, and love him. Those who hated him would grit their teeth and employ every means against him, only to make him stronger and stronger.

He would walk his own path, solitude wrapping itself around him, his indifferent eyes seeing through every falsehood.

Faced with criticism and rejection, he would scoff with disdain. Faced with extravagant praise and flattery, he would sneer without a word.

He could endure solitude, even enjoy it.

Either he would move toward success, or toward destruction.

He would be a warrior, making a solitary pilgrimage through darkness, cutting through every obstacle. Nothing could stop him. If a god stood in his way, he would kill the god. If a Buddha stood in his way, he would kill the Buddha!

This dream remained buried deep in my heart. During these years of writing, I thought several times of beginning, but nothing came of it. I was a little afraid. I felt my writing was not yet good enough.

And so the years passed. I experienced a few things, and much had changed since I first began writing.

Some of the past was painful to look back on. The dream, too, became buried in the dust of time, gradually forgotten. Even when I occasionally remembered it, I felt insufficiently prepared, not yet capable of writing such a satisfying story.

But these past few days, I stopped moving forward and looked back. Suddenly, I understood something: life is not cooking. When you cook, you usually prepare the oil, salt, soy sauce, and vinegar before you begin.

Life does not come prepared. Even when ’everything is ready,’ is not one crucial condition still missing?

Life is always hurried. Many things happen suddenly, with little preparation, or none at all.

Fate changes unpredictably. No one can foresee what tomorrow will bring.

So I made a decision. Against the backdrop of the approaching, prophesied end of the world in 2012, I would write a new book.

Its title would be Gu Zhen Ren — Reverend Insanity.

No title could suit it better.

This is the fruit of a seed planted six years ago.

Giving the novel the same name has no other significance. It is not narcissism or arrogance, but simply an expression of fulfilling a dream. I want to realize that original dream. I want to recover the feeling that once moved me.

Undoubtedly, this is a book that will invite controversy. In the view of the LK crowd, this is what they call ’three views not upright.’

Yes, I frankly admit it here: this is a book with three views not upright.

Because the protagonist of this book is a great villain, who by any means necessary will never stop, supremely evil! While the villains in this book are mostly upright young heroes of great brilliance, or famous righteous sects of honor and glory.

I know this will disappoint many people, but please also understand — those who abandon their position the moment they see a beautiful woman, do they still have the air of a peerless demon lord, a great boss of the Demonic Path?

That is infatuation.

That is right, this book will kill women, and kill beautiful women, kill them in great numbers, kill them with abandon.

And it will never stay its hand. No matter what existence it is, as long as it is hostile. No matter how powerful he is, it will contend with him. No matter how beautiful she is, it will kill her just the same.

This book is very evil. Read with caution.

Reading, after all, is a hobby for pleasure.

Reading should be for fun. If you truly are not happy, if reading it makes you unhappy, then do not read it, change to another book. There are a great many good books on Qidian, and I will recommend one or two as I write.

Those who cannot continue, who feel unhappy, who feel their own view of life, values, and morals have been trampled — you have my respect and you may leave!

Those who want to read are naturally welcome.

The above is the preface to this book, and also a declaration.
