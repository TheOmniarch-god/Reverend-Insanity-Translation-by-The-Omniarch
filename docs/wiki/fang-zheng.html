Who he is

Fang Zheng is Fang Yuan's younger brother, raised in the same house under the same name, and living in the shadow of a boy everyone had already written off.

The novel gives him one physical habit that does the work of a page of description.

Fang Zheng lowered his head, looking down at his toes out of long habit.

Chapter 2, paragraph 30

Over the years the habit deepened rather than faded.

Over the years, Fang Zheng’s head had hung lower and lower, and he had grown increasingly silent.

Chapter 2, paragraph 35

The first thing that changes

When his brother returns from death altered, calmer, colder, and watchful, Fang Zheng is the first to feel it, before anyone has proof of anything.

Fang Zheng felt a sudden tremor in his heart, feeling that his brother’s gaze was as sharp as an icy blade, seeming to pierce into the deepest part of his heart.

Chapter 2, paragraph 59

Why he matters

Every reader enters this novel through Fang Yuan, and Fang Yuan is a monster. Fang Zheng is the counterweight: the ordinary boy in the same house, who has done nothing wrong and inherited nothing, watching the world rearrange itself around his brother.

He is also the reason Shen Cui matters, for when he sees her fussing over Fang Yuan, the envy is unmistakable.

When Fang Zheng came in, he saw Shen Cui smoothing the creases in Fang Yuan’s clothes over his chest, and a glint of envy and jealousy involuntarily flickered in his eyes.

Chapter 3, paragraph 14
