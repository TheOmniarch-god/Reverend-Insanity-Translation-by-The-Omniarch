Author's Preface

Either Toward Success or Toward Destruction · Gu Zhen Ren

Front matter · Reverend Insanity · Translator: The Omniarch · 1,454 words · ~7 min read
Typeface
← →

Over the past few months, I have experienced the pain life has brought me, struggling through it in both body and mind.

Life is like a balance: on one side lies “reality,” and on the other, “the inner self.” For me, the side holding reality grew heavier and heavier, throwing the balance out of equilibrium and causing pain.

People often travel so long, losing themselves in the scenery along the way, that they forget why they set out in the first place. That was how it was for me. Amid the pain, I repeatedly felt lost.

During this rest between finishing my last book and the present, I have been trying to find my direction—to recover the reason I first set out.

It was about five or six years ago. By then, I had been reading novels for some time. The first was The Ethereal Journey, followed by Blasphemy, Slaying Immortals, Mad God, The Beast-Taming Studio, Boiling Beast Blood, Mount Shu, I Alone Am Immortal, and others.

I read many, many novels, but frustration accumulated within me. Most of the protagonists I encountered enjoyed extraordinary good fortune and were upright and virtuous. Most villains, meanwhile, were foolish, crazed, and ugly. Their reputations were impressive, but they were paper tigers: imposing enough until they met the protagonist, when they wilted.

So I began to think: I wanted to see a genuine archvillain.

This villain would go his own way—proud, solitary, and dark; decisive in killing, never losing his nerve. He would never conceal his evil, and there would be no pretense of goodness.

He would look unmistakably evil, with a formidable demonic presence and murderous intent all about him. What he did best would be trampling rules and slaughtering the renowned powers of the Righteous Path.

Alone, he would stand against the entire world, spreading terror among all living things. He would be a great master of the Demonic Path: sometimes arrogant and overbearing, sometimes sinister and cunning, sometimes cold and merciless. He would stand proudly at the summit, scornful of the world, and every brave soul who challenged him would meet a miserable end.

His methods would be cruel. Perhaps he would even handle Gu—in short, one look would tell you he was no good.

Then what would his name be… Super Demon King? Old Black Mountain Demon? Green-Robed Ancestor?

No, no, no.

After thinking it over, I decided: why not call him Gu Zhen Ren?

As it happened, I needed to create a Qidian account at the time. Okay—and so that username was born.

Some television dramas often play out like this: the villain defeats the protagonist and needs only one last blow to kill him. Then the villain starts rambling. Meanwhile, the protagonist secretly gathers strength, makes a final desperate effort, and invariably turns the tables to win a complete victory.

If the villain is the one at a disadvantage, the scene goes like this instead: after the protagonist unleashes his power, the defeated villain lies sprawled on the ground. Facing the protagonist’s sword, he suddenly bursts into tears and swears to mend his ways. The “kindhearted” protagonist hesitates. The villain seizes the moment to counterattack—and fails. The protagonist, “seeing through everything with perfect clarity,” thoroughly subdues him. His heart hardens, he shouts, “Despicable, shameless scum!” and finally kills the villain.

Then there are villains who kidnap the protagonist’s friends and family but almost never succeed in using them as leverage. Even with an enormous advantage, they somehow lose to the protagonist through all manner of circumstances beyond their control.

It was about six years ago that I created the username Gu Zhen Ren, with a wish in my heart: to witness an evil archvillain.

He would have his own way of doing things. Rejected by the world, he would act freely and without restraint, disdaining explanation. He would be decisive in killing, never entangled by affection, and never weak at the knees before a beautiful woman.

His fearsome reputation would make the world tremble; people would pale at the mention of him.

Those who understood him would admire, revere, and love him. Those who hated him would grit their teeth and employ every means against him, only to make him stronger and stronger.

He would walk his own path, surrounded by solitude, his indifferent eyes seeing through every falsehood.

He would scoff at criticism and rejection. Faced with extravagant praise and flattery, he would sneer without a word.

He could endure solitude—even enjoy it.

He would either move toward success or toward destruction.

He would be a warrior, making a solitary pilgrimage through the darkness, cutting through every obstacle. Nothing could stop him. If a god stood in his way, he would kill the god; if a Buddha stood in his way, he would kill the Buddha!

This dream remained buried deep in my heart. During my years of writing, I thought several times of beginning, but nothing came of it. I was a little afraid; I felt my writing was not yet good enough.

And so the years passed. I experienced a few things, and much had changed since I first began writing.

Some of the past was painful to look back on. The dream, too, became buried in the dust of time, gradually forgotten. Even when I occasionally remembered it, I felt insufficiently prepared, not yet capable of writing such a satisfying story.

But these past few days, I stopped moving forward and looked back. Suddenly, I understood something: life is not cooking. When you cook, you usually prepare the oil, salt, soy sauce, and vinegar before you begin.

Life does not come prepared. Even when “everything is ready,” isn’t one crucial condition still missing?

Life is always hurried. Many things happen suddenly, with little preparation—or none at all. Fate changes unpredictably; no one can foresee what tomorrow will bring.

So I made a decision. Against the backdrop of the approaching, prophesied end of the world in 2012, I would write a new book.

Its title would be Gu Zhen Ren—Reverend Insanity.

No title could suit it better.

This is the fruit of a seed planted six years ago.

Giving the novel the same name has no other significance. It is not narcissism or arrogance, but simply an expression of fulfilling a dream. I want to realize that original dream. I want to recover the feeling that once moved me.

Therefore, this book will be very different from other books, and its style will differ greatly from my previous work.

The protagonist is an archvillain who will use any means and stop at nothing to achieve his goals—supremely evil! Most of the antagonists, meanwhile, will be “great, glorious, and upright” young heroes and prodigies, or reputable, honored sects of the Righteous Path.

This book will contain scenes like these: the protagonist defeats one of those paragons of righteousness and, without a word of unnecessary talk, extinguishes his opponent’s chance of a comeback with a single stroke. He will also kidnap their friends and family, forcing these paragons to back down.

And he will show no mercy, whatever the enemy may be. However powerful they are, he will resist them. However beautiful she is, he will kill her all the same.

Yes, this book will kill women—and beautiful women at that. It will kill them without holding back.

Those who go weak in all three legs at the sight of a beauty, whose eyes fill with hearts, or who look forward to romantic encounters are not what this book is here to portray.

I know this will disappoint many people. But please understand: does someone who abandons his position the moment he sees a beauty still have the bearing of a great demon, an archvillain of the evil path?

That is merely being girl-crazy.

In this book, I want to portray an archvillain’s madness, rebellion, ruthlessness, unrestrained behavior, and reckless disregard.

Without question, this book will provoke controversy. In the language of the Long Kong forum community, this is called having “warped values.”

Yes, I openly admit it here: this is a book with warped values.

Reading is a pleasure and a pastime.

This book is very evil. Read with caution.

Those who want to read it are welcome, of course.

Those who cannot continue, who find no enjoyment in it, who feel that their outlook on life, values, and moral outlook have been trampled upon—I respectfully bid you farewell!

Reading is meant to be enjoyable. If you truly find no happiness in it, if you are not enjoying yourself, then stop reading and choose another book. Qidian has many excellent books, and I will recommend some as I write.

The above is both this book’s preface and its declaration.
