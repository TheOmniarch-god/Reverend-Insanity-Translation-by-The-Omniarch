# Hanzi Intent vs English: Brow Furrow vs Frown

**All are 眉头/皱眉/蹙眉/拧成/凝成疙瘩 — all refer to BROW, not mouth.**

## English Distinction (research)

- **Furrowed brow (singular)** = forehead wrinkled, physical action of brow/forehead. Cambridge Dictionary example "a furrowed brow". Collins "furrow one's brow". This matches hanzi 眉头/皱眉 literally — physical brow action.
- **Furrowed brows (plural)** = eyebrows pulled together, also physical, but plural refers to eyebrows themselves.
- **Frown** = full facial expression: brow furrow + mouth downturn, indicates disapproval/displeasure/sadness. Oxford: frown characterized by furrowing of brows. So frown INCLUDES brow furrow but adds mouth.
- **Knitted brows** = older idiom, to contract brows into wrinkles, synonym for furrowed brows.

**Therefore: Chinese 眉头... = furrowed brow/brows, NOT frown.** Frown adds mouth which hanzi does NOT mention. So furrowed brow is more accurate to hanzi intent.

## Contextual Intent

- Deep thought / worry / concentration (Ch2 thinking 500 years, Ch8 worrying about stones): brow furrowed deeper / brow furrowed slightly — physical thought.
- Disapproval / disappointment (Ch4 clan leader seeing no aptitude, Ch5负责人 seeing few Hope Gu, Ch6 academy elder hearing laughter, Ch7 academy elder hearing murmurs, Ch9 uncle seeing wine): Chinese still says brow furrow, but English could interpret as frown (disapproval). However hanzi intent is still brow furrow — the emotion is conveyed via brow, not mouth. So more accurate to hanzi is brow furrow, not frown.

## Proposed Consistent Standard Matching Hanzi Intent

1. **Slight furrow (微微皱眉 / 皱起眉头 / 蹙眉)**: "furrowed his brow slightly" / "faintly furrowed his brow" / "brow furrowed slightly" — singular brow, slight.
2. **Deep furrow (越皱越深 / 深皱着眉头)**: "brow furrowed deeper" / "brow furrowed deeply" — singular, deep.
3. **Deep knot/lump (拧成了疙瘩 / 凝成了一个疙瘩)**: "brow furrowed deeply" or "brow knotted together" or "brow furrowed into a deep knot" — keep knot image if we want literal hanzi, but standard English would be "brow furrowed deeply". To match hanzi intent (knot = deep furrow), use "brow furrowed deeply" or "brow knotted together".
4. **Raised brows (扬起眉头)**: "raised his brows slightly" — plural, correct for raising eyebrows.
5. **Multiple people (有的皱起眉头)**: "some furrowed their brows" — plural possessive for multiple persons, verb furrowed.

**Therefore: Replace all "frowned" that came from 眉头/皱眉/蹙眉 with "brow furrowed" versions to match hanzi intent.**

- Ch4: "frowned slightly" → "brow furrowed slightly" or "furrowed his brow slightly" (from 蹙眉)
- Ch5: "frowned" (twice) → "furrowed his brow" / "brow furrowed"
- Ch6: "frowned deeply" → "brow furrowed deeply" (from 深皱着眉头)
- Ch7: "frown deepened" → "brow furrowed deeper" or "brow furrowed into a deep knot" (from 凝成了一个疙瘩) — but "frown deepened" is interpretive, not hanzi accurate
- Keep "frown" only if Chinese was something like 脸色一沉 or no brow mention, but we have none.
