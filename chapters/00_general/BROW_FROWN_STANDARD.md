# Brow vs Brows vs Frown — Research & Standard

## Web sources
- Cambridge Dictionary: "a furrowed brow" [https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/furrowed] — singular brow is idiomatic example.
- Collins: "to furrow one's brow" — verb + singular brow.
- Vocabulary.com: "furrowed brow" most common meaning is deep line on face.
- StackExchange English: FROWN = facial expression indicating disapproval, characterized by furrowing of one's brows. Knitted brows = to contract one's brows into wrinkles. Frowning = to contract brow in displeasure.
- HiNative: furrow eyebrows = wrinkling eyebrows together; frown = mouth downturn, often both together.
- WordReference: brow = forehead (above eyebrow), more common with furrow than forehead. "Furrowed brow" common, "furrowed forehead" not heard.

## Distinction
- **brow (singular)**: forehead region, or collective singular for one person's brow. Idiomatic standard: "furrow one's brow", "his brow furrowed", "a furrowed brow". Use for thinking, worry, concentration. Tolkien style prefers singular.
- **brows (plural)**: eyebrows themselves (the hair). Use when action is specifically eyebrows: "raised his brows" (correct plural), "furrowed his brows" (eyebrows pulled together). Grammatically okay but less idiomatic than singular for furrow; Cambridge uses singular.
- **frown (noun/verb)**: full expression including brow furrow + mouth. "frowned", "frown deepened", "frowned slightly" = disapproval, displeasure, disappointment.
- **knitted brows**: older idiom, synonym for furrowed brows, means contracted into wrinkles. Valid but archaic, less common now. Should be replaced with furrowed for consistency unless intentional archaic flavour.
