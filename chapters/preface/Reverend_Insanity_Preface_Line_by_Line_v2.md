### L01

**Original:** 在过去的几个月内，我经历了生活赐予的痛苦，并且身心都在其中挣扎。

**Literal:** In past few months inside, I experienced life bestows pain, and body-mind both in among it struggle.

**Judgment:** Fix: 'a kind of pain life bestows' is Chinglish. 经历 = live through / go through / endure. 生活赐予 = life bestows (赐予 formal, keeps agency to life). 身心 = body and mind (or body and soul). 都在其中挣扎 = both struggling within it — keep 'both' for 都, 'within it' for 其中. Better rhythm: 'with body and mind both struggling through it' avoids double 'within'. Alternative considered: 'physically and mentally struggling' loses 身心 noun weight.

**Proposal:** In the past few months, I have lived through pain bestowed by life, with body and mind both struggling through it.

In the past few months, I have lived through pain bestowed by life, with body and mind both struggling through it.

Life is like a balance scale, with 'reality' on one end and 'the inner self' on the other. For me, the reality end grew heavier and heavier, the scale tipped out of balance, and pain followed.

People walk a road for so long that they lose themselves in the scenery along the way, and often forget why they set out in the first place.

That is what happened. In that pain I felt waves of bewilderment.

Since finishing my last book until now, during this rest, I have been trying to find direction, trying to recover the reason I set out in the first place.

That was five or six years ago. I had already been reading novels for a while by then. The first was A Journey to the Ethereal, then Blasphemy, Zhu Xian, Mad God, The Beast-Taming House, Boiling Beast Blood, Mount Shu, I Alone Am Immortal, and so on.

I had read a great many novels, but a knot of frustration built up in my chest. The protagonists I saw were mostly blessed with heaven-defying fortune, upright and noble in character. The villains were mostly foolish, deranged, and ugly, their titles resounding, but they were all paper tigers — impressive to look at until they met the protagonist, then they wilted.

So I started to think — I wanted to see a true archvillain.

This villain would walk his own path, proud, solitary, and dark, decisive in slaughter, never weak in the knees. He would never hide his own evil, and would have no false goodness whatsoever.

You would know at a glance he was utterly evil — demonic aura stark, killing intent thick in the air. What he was best at was trampling rules and slaughtering the famed Righteous Path.

Alone, against the whole world, spreading terror to all living beings. He would be a great grandmaster of the Demonic Path — sometimes arrogant and overbearing, sometimes sinister and cunning, sometimes cold and merciless — standing proudly at the summit, scorning the world with evil eyes, and every brave soul who dared challenge him would meet a miserable end.

His methods cruel, perhaps toying with Gu worms — in short, you could tell at a glance he was no good at all.

So what would his name be... Super Demon King? Black Mountain Old Demon? Green-Robed Ancestor?

No, no, no.

After thinking it over, why not call him Gu Zhen Ren?

Some TV dramas always go like this: the archvillain defeats the protagonist, just one final lethal strike away. Then the villain starts rambling on and on, while the protagonist secretly gathers strength for one last gamble — and every single time he turns the tables and wins a complete victory.

Facing the protagonist's sword, the villain suddenly bursts into tears, wailing and swearing to mend his ways. The 'kindhearted and benevolent' protagonist hesitates. At that moment the villain launches a desperate counterattack — and fails. The protagonist, 'seeing every autumn hair, seeing through fire,' completely suppresses him, his heart hardening at the same time, shouting 'Despicable! Shameless!
