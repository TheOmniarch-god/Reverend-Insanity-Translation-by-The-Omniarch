# Chapter 1: Though the Flesh Perish, the Demonic Heart Knows No Regret
### Discovery and Lore — What Chapter 1 Actually Gives You

*Public Reader's Guide — No spoilers beyond Chapter 1. This is what you learn if you read slowly.*

#### The Opening That Starts At The End

Reverend Insanity does not start with a boy who wants to become strong. It starts with a man who already became the strongest, and lost.

Fang Yuan stands alone on a mountaintop, wearing a tattered emerald robe, hair wild, body covered in hundreds of wounds. Blood has already formed a pool at his feet. Around him, the entire Righteous Path has gathered. They have laid what the text calls an inescapable dragnet. For six hours, no one dares to move.

That first image tells you the power scale of this world. One demon can make the righteous world afraid to take a single step.

He does not beg. He recites a poem about green mountains and white hair, laughs, and detonates himself.

Then the rain starts. And we are five hundred years in the past.

That cut, from apocalypse to quiet village rain, is Gu Zhen Ren's signature. Grand, then intimate.

#### Huaxia — Why The Live Text Uses Huaxia, And Why It Matters

In the live version of Chapter 1, you will read this line:

> He had originally been a Huaxia student on Earth...

We kept Huaxia on purpose, and it is correct.

Many translations flatten Huaxia to Chinese. That loses meaning.

Huaxia, written as 华夏, is not the modern political name Zhongguo, which means China. Huaxia is the oldest poetic name for the civilization itself, used for more than three thousand years.

Hua means splendid, luminous, cultural brilliance. Xia means grand, great, from the Xia dynasty, the first dynasty.

Put together, Huaxia means the splendid and grand civilization. It speaks of culture, ritual, poetry, and heritage, not a passport or a border.

When Gu Zhen Ren says Fang Yuan is from Huaxia, he is telling you three things.

First, Fang Yuan carries five thousand years of cultural memory. Second, his ability to quote Li Bai and Yang Shen without thinking comes from that root. Third, he is a transmigrator, but not a blank slate. He is culturally Chinese to the bone.

That is why his poems matter later in the chapter. That is why the elders think a child who can compose Tang poetry and Song lyrics at age five must have great aptitude. In this worldview, literary talent and cultivation talent are linked.

Note for readers: In an earlier draft we used Chinese student. The live master now uses Huaxia student, which is the right call, and we added this note so new readers understand the weight.

Li Bai, who is quoted in this chapter, was a poet of the Tang Dynasty who wrote many poems that are still memorized today. He lived in the eighth century and is considered an immortal poet.

#### Spring Autumn Cicada — Time Is A Gu

The object everyone is killing for is called Spring Autumn Cicada, or Chun Qiu Chan.

Chapter 1 tells you exactly what it is.

It is Rank Seven, and it is one of the Ten Great Mystical Gu. In a world where most Gu Masters never reach Rank Five, Rank Seven is mythic. That rank alone explains why the whole world came to kill Fang Yuan.

Its ability is to reverse time. Not to travel to the future, but to send the will back.

Its cost is tens of millions of lives. The text says Fang Yuan slaughtered tens of millions of innocents to refine it. That number is important to keep separate from the village description later. When Fang Yuan is fifteen again, the text says Gu Yue Village has not ten thousand households, but several thousand nonetheless. That is the size of a mountain village. Tens of millions is the scale of his sin as a Demon Lord five hundred years later. One is present, small and intimate. The other is future, vast and terrible.

Why is it called Spring Autumn Cicada? Spring and Autumn refers to the Spring and Autumn Annals, the ancient chronicle where Chinese history was first recorded as moral history. A cicada is an insect that lives a short life, from spring to autumn, yet in Chinese culture it symbolizes rebirth because it sheds its skin. A Gu named after a short lived insect that spans the seasons and hears history being recorded, and can reverse history itself, is pure Gu Zhen Ren naming. The Gu is its philosophy.

Fang Yuan's last vow before exploding links directly to this:

> If this newly refined Spring Autumn Cicada works, I will still be a demon in my next life!

He does not wish to be good in the next life. He chooses the same path again.

And when he wakes up at fifteen, the text says:

> Gu Yue Village... this is five hundred years ago?! The Spring Autumn Cicada really had an effect...

We use had an effect on purpose in the live version, not was effective. Was effective sounds like a lab report. Had an effect sounds like a fifteen year old boy feeling his young palm and realising a myth actually worked. It links wonder to result. That wording is intentional and matches his age and shock.

#### The Poem Before Death

Before he dies, Fang Yuan recites:

> Green mountains beneath the setting sun,
> Autumn moon and spring breeze.
> In the morning one's hair is like black silk,
> By dusk it turns to snow.
> Right and wrong, success and failure,
> All vanish in a turn of the head.

Two of those lines are borrowed, and that is deliberate.

In the morning one's hair is like black silk, by dusk it turns to snow comes from Li Bai, on how fast life passes.

Right and wrong, success and failure, all vanish in a turn of the head comes from Yang Shen's Lin Jiang Xian, the poem that opens Romance of the Three Kingdoms, China's most famous novel about ambition and emptiness.

Fang Yuan, a Huaxia student, quotes the two ultimate poems about impermanence as he dies. He is not sad. He is summarizing five hundred years. Black hair to white, success to dust, in one turn of the head.

This chapter is about time, not power. Power is the vehicle. Time is the subject.

#### The Demonic Path — No Regret

Chapter 1 gives the only definition you need, from Fang Yuan's inner voice:

> For what was the Demonic Path? It was to accumulate no good karma, only murder and arson; spurned by heaven and reviled by earth, making enemies of the whole world, and still running wild, following the whim of one's own heart to the very end.

A note on wording: the live text now says making enemies of the whole world. Earlier drafts had making enemies with the whole world. Formal literary prose prefers making an enemy of or making enemies of, because you make friends with someone, but you make an enemy of someone. The live version is already corrected to making enemies of the whole world, which is the right literary form.

And the key line that gives the chapter its title:

> A trace of wistfulness stirred, yet not the faintest whisper of regret.

The flesh can perish. The choice is not regretted. That is the whole philosophy in one sentence.

#### Qing Mao Mountain and Gu Yue Village

After the explosion, we wake in rain.

Qing Mao Mountain means Green Thatch Mountain. Not a grand sacred mountain, a thatch covered hill. Humble.

Gu Yue Village means Ancient Moon Village. Gu Yue is also the clan name. Notice the characters. Gu means Gu, the insect, the essence. Yue means Moon. The clan that raises Gu under the moon.

That name may be the secret to their cultivation. Perhaps this is the secret cultivation method that gives them the Moonlight Gu, which appears later. The clan lives under the moon, raises Gu under moonlight, and their signature Gu is Moonlight Gu. The name tells you the method.

Details Chapter 1 quietly gives you:

The lights at night come from stilt houses. These are southern mountain houses built on tall wooden pillars because the ground is uneven. The text says they number not ten thousand households, but several thousand nonetheless.

You are right that starting a sentence with not ten thousand households feels awkward for audio and for article flow. A more literary rendering that matches our Chapter 8 standard would be: These lights came from the stilt houses, numbering not ten thousand households, but several thousand nonetheless. Or: The lights came from stilt houses, several thousand households, not ten thousand, but enough to drape the slope in light. We should update Chapters One through Seven to the literary standard we achieved in Chapter Eight before we record the progress video, so the whole early arc reads consistently.

The ancestral hall has amber floorboards, a black lacquered altar with three tiers, spirit tablets, and red copper incense burners with thin smoke coiling upward. You can smell it. The clan leader and elders kneel and kowtow, foreheads thudding softly. Ritual matters here.

#### Why Aperture Opening Ceremony, Not Awakening

The text calls it Aperture Opening Ceremony, not Aperture Awakening Ceremony, and that choice is precise.

In Chinese it is Kai Qiao, which means to open the aperture. Every person has an aperture, called Kong Qiao, located three inches below the navel, between the kidneys. It is a space that holds primeval essence. At fifteen, Hope Gu opens it. It already exists. You are not awakening consciousness. You are opening a physical and spiritual space.

Awakening in Chinese would be Jue Xing, which is about consciousness waking up. Opening is Kai, which is about a door being opened. The ceremony is literally opening a door in the body.

That is why it is called Opening.

#### Grades and Why Three Years Without Grade A Is A Crisis

Chapter 1 introduces the talent system:

Grade A, Grade B, Grade C, Grade D. Not just letters, a caste.

The text says: Sigh, I only hope a youth with Grade A aptitude emerges. It has been three years since our Gu Yue clan had such a genius.

Why is three years a crisis? Because Gu Yue is competing with Bai Village and Xiong Village. Bai Village has Bai Ning Bing, who reached Rank Three Gu Master in just two short years. That is terrifying. Even the older generation feels pressure.

A clan's strength comes from bloodline. A strong ancestor improves the bloodline, which improves children's aptitude. The text notes: Four generations of clan leader was the only Rank Five, but died with Flower Wine Monk without leaving bloodline. So blood is thinning. No Grade A for three years means the clan is weakening, no future pillar, no future clan leader candidate. In ten years, a Grade A can become Rank Four and compete for clan leader. No Grade A means no future.

That is why the elders fight until their faces flush red and heads bleed to recruit geniuses.

#### The Poems Fang Yuan Composed As A Child

The elders gossip: He could speak at three months and walk at four. By age five, he could compose Tang poetry and Song lyrics.

They name three:

Bring in the Wine, by Li Bai, Tang Dynasty, arguably the most famous drinking poem about time and ambition.

Ode to the Plum Blossom, which has many famous versions. The most famous are by Lu You of the Song Dynasty, with the line Even when crushed into mud and dust, its fragrance remains the same, and by Wang Mian of the Yuan and Ming transition, who wrote about plum blossoms in ink. Both use plum blossom to symbolize resilience in winter. Fang Yuan composing this at five tells you he identifies with endurance.

Jiang Cheng Zi, by Su Shi, also called Su Dongpo, Song Dynasty, a famous lyric about longing and memory.

Not naming the authors in the gossip is intentional. In the village, everyone knows these poems. For us as readers, knowing Li Bai is Tang, Su Shi is Song, and Wang Mian and Lu You are Song and Yuan, tells you Fang Yuan's literary memory spans dynasties.

#### Three Hundred Years — Drifting or Struggling?

You noted you hated the word drifting, and you are right. The live version now says:

> Three hundred years of wandering and struggle; two hundred years of unbridled dominion...

The older draft said Three hundred years of drifting, tossed like flotsam upon tempestuous tides.

In Chinese, the phrase is often San Bai Nian Piao Bo, where Piao Bo means to drift, to wander, to be tossed about, to have no fixed home. It can be translated as drifting, but drifting feels passive, like flotsam. Fang Yuan was not passive. He was struggling.

Wandering and struggle captures both. Wandering captures the root of Piao Bo, the lack of home. Struggle captures the active hardship. That is why we changed it in the live master, and the live master is what you see on the site. The live is our master.

#### What Chapter 1 Really Teaches

If you had to summarize Chapter 1 in one discovery, it is this:

Chapter 1 is about a man who had everything, lost it, and chose the same path again, but earlier.

It is not about a second chance to be good. It is about a second chance to be more efficiently yourself.

Huaxia explains his soul. Spring Autumn Cicada explains his method. The poems explain his worldview. Gu Yue Village explains his starting point. And the elders' gossip explains the world that will try to use him.

That is why Chapter 1 is called Though the Flesh Perish, the Demonic Heart Knows No Regret. The flesh perished on that mountaintop. The heart did not.
