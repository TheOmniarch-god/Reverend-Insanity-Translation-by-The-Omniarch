### L00

> 空窍玄妙异常，虽然寄托在方源体内，但是却不和五脏六腑同处一个空间。你可以说它无限大，又可以说它无限小。

**Literal:** The aperture is strange and profound beyond the ordinary. Although it is lodged inside Fang Yuan’s body, it does not sit in the same space as the five zang and six fu organs. You can call it infinitely big, you can call it infinitely small.

**Draft:** The aperture was a thing of surpassing mystery. Though it was lodged within Fang Yuan’s body, it did not share the same space as his internal organs. You could call it infinitely vast, and you could just as well call it infinitely small.

五脏六腑 is the Chinese medical inventory of organs. Spelling it out in English turns a throwaway phrase into a lecture, so I let it mean what it means in use, the internal organs. Both halves of the paradox have to survive. Han Dian has 空窍 as a real anatomical word, the openings of the body, quoting Han Feizi, so aperture sits at the right register and is not a coinage.

*I checked: [Han Dian, 空窍](https://www.zdic.net/hans/%E7%A9%BA%E7%AA%8D)*

### L01

> 有人称之为紫府，有人称之为华池。不过更多人称之为元海空窍。

**Literal:** Some call it Purple Palace, some call it Flowery Pool. But more call it the Primeval Sea Aperture.

**Draft:** Some called it the Purple Palace. Others called it the Flowery Pool. Most, however, called it the Primeval Sea Aperture.

Both names are real internal alchemy vocabulary, and my first note had the wrong sense of 华池. Han Dian leads with the mouth, the hollow under the tongue, which is a genuine sense but not the one operating here. The neidan diagram tradition gives the other: 脐后肾前有一穴叫炎海，里面有华池，古人称为下田，乃藏精之所, behind the navel and in front of the kidneys is a cavity called the Qi Sea, and inside it the Flowery Pool, which the ancients called the lower field, where essence is stored. That is the exact location already published for the aperture in Chapter 5, below the navel and between the kidneys. 紫府 works the same way: Baidu Baike gives one reading as the dwelling of immortals and the other as 人身体内修炼的关窍, the cultivation aperture inside the body, and neidan glossaries list it among the standing aliases of the upper dantian. So the author is not decorating. He is offering two established names for a cultivation cavity, one usually upper and one usually lower. They stay capitalised.

*I checked: [Han Dian, 紫府](https://www.zdic.net/hans/%E7%B4%AB%E5%BA%9C), [Han Dian, 华池](https://www.zdic.net/hans/%E5%8D%8E%E6%B1%A0), [Baidu Baike, 紫府](https://baike.baidu.com/item/%E7%B4%AB%E5%BA%9C/10439843), [Neidan diagram commentary, 华池 at 脐后肾前](https://www.yzthome.com/thread-1856.htm), [Internal alchemy glossary listing 紫府 among upper-dantian aliases](https://www.taijizhe.com/topic.php?vid=543)*

### L02

> 整个空窍呈球形，空窍表面流动着白光，是一层光膜。就是先前的希望蛊炸裂开来，凝聚的一层光。

**Literal:** The whole aperture takes a ball shape. White light flows on its surface, a layer of light membrane. It is the Hope Gu from before, burst open, a layer of light drawn together.

**Draft:** This was what the Hope Gu had become: burst apart, then drawn together into a single layer of light.

I started with congealed and changed it. Han Dian defines 凝结 as gas turning to liquid or liquid turning to solid, English condensation, with a second sense of gathering. Congeal pulls hard toward solidity, which is wrong for light and wrong for a sea. Condensed covers both, and I am holding it throughout. The sentence also read as a contradiction, bursting and condensing in one breath, so I have recast it as a sequence: what the Hope Gu became.

### L03

**Draft:** It was precisely this membrane of light that held the aperture together and kept it from collapsing.

### L05

> 海水平滑如镜，呈现一片碧青之色，却又浓稠无比，带着铜的光泽。

**Literal:** The seawater is smooth as a mirror, showing an expanse of jade-green, yet thick beyond compare, carrying the lustre of copper.

**Draft:** The water lay smooth as a mirror, a sheet of jade green, yet viscous beyond any water, and it carried the sheen of copper.

浓稠 has no headword of its own in Han Dian, but the characters are 浓, dense, and 稠, thick in the sense of not flowing freely. Thick in English is ambiguous, it can mean deep or opaque. Viscous is the actual property, and it matters, because the reader needs to know this is not water. 铜 means copper here; the next line separately names 青铜 (bronze), so the colour terms should remain distinct. On the green and the bronze looking like a contradiction, they are not. 青铜 is named for its green. Han Dian gives bronze as 呈青灰色, greenish grey, and Chinese Wikipedia says outright 因为其氧化物颜色青灰，故名青铜, named for the greenish grey of its oxide. The Cantonese entry is blunter: fresh bronze is 古铜色, not green at all, and it is called 青铜 only because it corrodes to blue green 铜绿. And Han Dian’s 碧 is 青绿色的玉石, green jade, with 碧水 for green water. The description therefore gives two separate visual qualities: jade-green water and a coppery lustre. The following line independently identifies the primeval essence as bronze.

*I checked: [Han Dian, 青铜](https://www.zdic.net/hans/%E9%9D%92%E9%93%9C), [Han Dian, 碧](https://www.zdic.net/hans/%E7%A2%A7), [Chinese Wikipedia, 青铜时代, bronze named for its green oxide](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9D%92%E9%93%9C%E6%97%B6%E4%BB%A3), [Cantonese Wikipedia, 青銅, fresh bronze is not green](https://zh-yue.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9D%92%E9%8A%85), [Ifeng, bronzes named for their green rust, quoting the Shuowen on 碧](https://gs.ifeng.com/c/8qaDXweU6rg)*

### L06

> 这是一转蛊师才有的青铜真元凝结，俗称青铜海。

**Literal:** This is the bronze primeval essence that only a Rank 1 Gu Master has, condensed. Commonly called the bronze sea.

**Draft:** This was the bronze primeval essence that only a Rank 1 Gu Master possessed, condensed together. Commonly, it was called the bronze sea.

真元 is not a coinage. Han Dian gives it as the root vital energy of the body, with a Tang citation, and ties it to the original qi stored by the kidneys. Primeval essence keeps it locked to 元海, primeval sea, which is the same 元.

### L07

> 海面不到空窍的一半高度，只有四成四。这也是丙等资质的局限。

**Literal:** The sea surface does not reach half the aperture’s height, only four tenths and four. This is also the limit of a Grade C aptitude.

**Draft:** The surface of the sea did not reach half the height of the aperture. It stood at only forty-four percent. This too was the limit of a Grade C aptitude.

四成四 is forty-four percent, and it attaches to 高度, height. So this is a fill level in a sphere, not a volume.

### L08

> 每一滴的海水，都是真元，代表着方源精气神的凝结，象征着他十五年来积蓄出来的生命潜能。

**Literal:** Every drop of the seawater is primeval essence, representing the condensing of Fang Yuan’s essence, qi and spirit, symbolising the life potential he has stored up across fifteen years.

**Draft:** Every drop of that water was primeval essence. It represented the condensing of Fang Yuan’s essence, qi and spirit, and it stood for the vital potential he had accumulated over fifteen years.

精气神 is a fixed Daoist triad. Han Dian glosses it flatly as vitality, but separates the three underneath: 精 the refined substance in tangible form, 气 the same in intangible form, 神 the living activity of both. Collapsing it to vitality throws away a structure the book will use again, so I keep three words.

### L11

### L12

> “一如前世啊。”对这个结果他淡然一笑。

**Literal:** ’Just as in the previous life.’ At this result he gave a 淡然 smile.

**Draft:** “Just as in my previous life.” He gave a faint, indifferent smile at the result.

Han Dian defines 淡然 as 漫不经心、毫不在意的样子, offhand and not minding in the slightest, English indifferently, coldly. A second gloss adds tranquil and calm. Both readings exist, but the core is not caring. Calm on its own is too neutral, it could just mean composed. Indifferent is the actual weight, and it is the point of the chapter.

### L13

> “不能再走了吗？”学堂家老抱着万分之一的希望，隔岸喊道。

**Literal:** ’Can you go no further?’ The academy elder, holding on to a one ten-thousandth hope, shouted from across the bank.

**Draft:** “Can you go no further?” The academy elder called from across the river, still clinging to that one chance in ten thousand.

My first justification for keeping the number was bad. Saying the clan’s hopes are the chapter’s subject explains nothing about why a numeral should survive. The real reason is narrower. 万分之一 is a stock intensifier, the same move as one in a million, meaning vanishingly small rather than a real probability. English has that move available, so nothing is lost by keeping a numeral, while the faintest hope quietly drops a figure the author did write.

### L15

> 这下子，就连那些少年们也反应过来了。

**Literal:** At this, even those youths reacted over.

**Draft:** At that, even the youths finally caught on.

反应 alone is to react. But the verb here is 反应过来, and the 过来 does the work, marking arrival at a state. The unit means the penny drops. So reacted would be wrong, and understood is right but flat, since it loses the slowness. Caught on carries both: they got there, and they got there late.

### L16

> 顿时，就听嗡的一声，人群炸开了锅。

**Literal:** At once, a 嗡 sound was heard, and the crowd exploded like a boiling pot.

**Draft:** At once there was a rising buzz, and the crowd erupted.

嗡 is a drone or buzz, and it sits inside the sentence rather than standing alone, so it does not break the rule I set about isolated sound words. 炸开了锅 is set, the pot boils over, used of a crowd going up in noise. Rendering the pot literally would be gibberish.

### L20

> 人群中掀起轩然大波。

**Literal:** A towering great wave rose up among the crowd.

Han Dian gives 轩然大波 two senses, the literal high surging wave out of a Han Yu poem, and the figurative furore. ### L21

> “哥哥……”在人群中，古月方正抬起头来，震惊地看着趟河回来的方源。他不敢相信眼前的一幕，自己的哥哥居然只是一个丙等？

**Literal:** ’Elder brother…’ In the crowd, Gu Yue Fang Zheng lifted his head, looking in shock at Fang Yuan wading back across the river. He could not believe the scene in front of him. His own elder brother was actually only a Grade C?

**Draft:** “Brother…” In the crowd, Gu Yue Fang Zheng raised his head and stared in shock at Fang Yuan wading back across the river. He could not believe the scene before him. His own elder brother was only a Grade C?

Dropping elder everywhere was sloppy of me. It depends on position. 哥哥 as direct address is what a boy actually calls his brother, and English speakers say Brother, not Elder Brother, out loud. In narration the seniority is information, and English carries it without strain. So Brother when he speaks to him, his elder brother when the narrator refers to him.

### L25

> “可恶，居然只是个丙等！”古月族长暗捏双拳，深深地叹了一口气，失望之情溢于言表。

**Literal:** ’Detestable, actually only a Grade C!’ The Gu Yue Clan leader secretly clenched both fists, sighed deeply, his disappointment spilling past his words.

**Draft:** “Damn it. Only a Grade C!” The Gu Yue Clan leader clenched his fists in secret and let out a deep sigh. His disappointment was plain beyond any words.

### L34

> 方源依旧面无表情地走着，距离越来越近，他可以清晰地看到学堂家老沉重的表情，可以敏锐地觉察到上百位少年投来的目光。

### L37

> 那时自己感觉天都塌下来了，过河时失足摔了一跤，浑身湿透，失魂落魄。却没有一个人搀扶自己。

**Literal:** At that time he felt the sky had collapsed. Crossing the river he lost his footing and fell, soaked through, soul lost and spirit fallen. Yet not one person held him up.

**Draft:** Back then he had felt as if the sky had come down. He had missed his footing crossing the river and gone sprawling, soaked through and out of his wits. And not one person had put out a hand to him.

失魂落魄 in Han Dian is the souls departing, used of someone shattered and running on autopilot. 搀扶 is specifically supporting someone by the arm, so the thing nobody did is physical, not emotional. That is colder, and better. This is also the hinge of the chapter, and it runs on a reversal. In the former life it was Fang Yuan who fell, 自己, and 却没有一个人攑扶自己, not one person put out a hand. In this life Fang Zheng falls and Fang Yuan is the hand. Note the cold detail that survives the reversal: nobody 攑扶’d Fang Yuan, and Fang Yuan does not 攑扶 Fang Zheng either. He 提s him, grips him by the collar the way one lifts a bag.

### L38

> 这些失望、冷漠的表情和眼神，像是尖刀凌迟着自己的心。思绪纷乱无比，胸口更是隐隐作痛。

**Literal:** These disappointed, cold expressions and looks were like sharp knives putting his heart to lingchi. His thoughts were tangled beyond compare, and his chest ached faintly.

**Draft:** Those disappointed, indifferent faces and eyes had been like sharp knives slicing his heart away piece by piece. His thoughts had run wild, and a dull ache had spread through his chest.

凌迟 is not generic cutting. Han Dian has it as the judicial execution by slow slicing, quoting the Song History on severing the limbs first and the throat last, and noting the popular name, a thousand knives and ten thousand cuts. So the English has to be incremental. I am not importing an image here, it is the verb on the page, and it is the most protracted killing the language had available.

### L47

### L58

> “哈哈，方家的难兄难弟！”岸上有人嘲笑出声。

**Draft:** “Haha, the Fang brothers, a matched pair of wretches!” Someone on the bank jeered aloud.

The hardest line here, and it is a tonal pun. Read in second tone the idiom is praise out of the Shishuo Xinyu, two brothers so equal that neither can be ranked above the other. The jeer activates the insult while the words still literally say brothers. English cannot hold both tones at once. ### L73

> “怎么可能？你赤练老鬼何德何能，误人子弟的本事倒是不错。这个孩子还不如交给我古月漠尘抚养。”古月漠尘像触电一般，立即咆哮起来。

**Literal:** ’How is it possible? You Chi Lian old ghost, what virtue and what ability have you? Your skill at ruining other people’s children is not bad, though. This child would be better handed to me, Gu Yue Mo Chen, to bring up.’ Gu Yue Mo Chen, as if electrified, immediately began to roar.

**Draft:** “How could that be? What virtue, what ability do you have, Chi Lian, you old geezer? Hampering other people’s children is the one talent you do have. Better to hand the child to me, Gu Yue Mo Chen, to bring up.” Gu Yue Mo Chen reacted as if a jolt had gone through him, and roared at once.

Old geezer. I went through old ghost and old wretch before landing here, and both were wrong for reasons worth recording. First the sourcing: CC-CEDICT, the database behind MDBG, Pleco and most dictionary apps, returns no entry at all for 老鬼, so there is no standard bilingual rendering to defer to. Wiktionary is the one bilingual dictionary that glosses it, and it gives derogatory gaffer, geezer for standard Mandarin, plus a separate Shanghainese sense of experienced, smart, astute. The 汉语大词典 entry, carried by several Chinese dictionary sites, says 方言。妻子称丈夫。多作为詈词或谑称, a term of abuse or a jesting appellation, refusing to fix the register. CantoDict has it as an old or experienced person, sometimes used in a cynical friendly way like old pal, and lists 死老鬼 separately as old bastard. That last detail decides the strength: a harsher form existed and the author did not use it, so the word is mid-register and the venom in this line comes from the clauses around it, not from 老鬼 itself. Old wretch therefore over-translated. Second, the form. 鬼 here is a pejorative agentive suffix, not a spirit, and dictionaries never carry it across literally: Han Dian renders 色鬼 as lounge lizard and satyr, not as a ghost of any kind. Old ghost is a calque that keeps a morpheme and loses the meaning, and in this book it carries a specific hazard, since the world actually contains souls and demons and a reader may take it as a description of what Chi Lian is. Geezer costs some period flavour and reads colloquial, but it behaves the way 老鬼 behaves, taking its force from context, and the scene is comic anyway: every elder present has lost his dignity and the clan leader is about to go into a frenzy. On the man himself, checked in the book rather than guessed: Chapter 4 gives Chi Lian 一头红发, a head of red hair, and calls him the 爷爷 of Gu Yue Chi Cheng, and in Chapter 21 he calls himself 爷爷. He is a man and a grandfather, so old bag and old hag are out as fact rather than taste. Chapter 4 also makes the two elders 老对头, long standing enemies heading the clan’s two largest factions with a grandson each in this ceremony, and establishes that Chi Lian falsified his own grandson’s aptitude, which makes the jab accidentally true. Hamper is Han Dian’s own English for 误人子弟. 何德何能 is normally self-deprecating, so turning it outward is what makes it a sneer. And 触电 has a figurative sense in Han Dian, 比喻突然受到强烈的刺激或震撼, a sudden violent stimulus, so it describes the onset of his reaction, not the roaring.

### L74

> “谁都别争了。这个孩子谁栽培，都没有本族长亲自栽培的好。谁有异议，就是反对我古月博！”古月族长双眼赤红，一扫先前的失望颓废，整个人都癫狂了。

**Literal:** ’Nobody argue. Whoever cultivates this child, none is as good as this clan leader cultivating him personally. Whoever has an objection, is opposing me, Gu Yue Bo!’ The Gu Yue Clan leader’s eyes were red, sweeping away his earlier disappointment and dejection, the whole person gone mad.

**Draft:** “None of you will contend for him. No matter who grooms this child, none will do it as well as this clan leader in person. Anyone with an objection is opposing me, Gu Yue Bo!” The Gu Yue Clan leader’s eyes were bloodshot, he had gone into a frenzy, his earlier disappointment and dejection were swept away.

Settled after a round I largely lost. I had objected that 谁都别争了 is an imperative and that None of you will contend for him was a prediction. Wrong: the commanding future is an imperative in English, and from a clan head taking control of a room it carries more authority than Stop contending, not less. I also objected to bloodshot on the grounds that 赤红 names a colour, crimson, while bloodshot names a condition. Merriam-Webster gives bloodshot as, of an eye, inflamed to redness, which is simply red eyes, so the distinction was too fine to be worth anything. Two criticisms of mine that did survive: 栽培 repeats in the Chinese, 谁栽培 then 亲自栽培的好, but grooms followed by grooming stumbles in English and do it carries the second instance; and 异议 and 反对 are two different words, dissent and oppose, where my earlier objects and objects invented a repetition the author did not write while losing the one he did. On the closing clauses, the Chinese order is 双眼赤红 then 一扫先前的失望颅废 then 整个人都癫狂了, ending on the madness. The published line moves the frenzy forward, which is the author’s beat reordered but reads better aloud, and the comma splice is doing breathless work that suits a man losing his composure. Disappointment and dejection is a compound subject, so were. 本族长 stays this clan leader: referring to himself by office is the authority claim the outburst rests on. 栽培 is Han Dian’s plant and tend, extended to training and fostering talent and then to patronage in old officialdom, which is why groom rather than raise, since L73 is 抚养, ordinary childrearing. And 癫狂 is a named disorder in Chinese medicine, derangement, plus a colloquial sense of being beside oneself.
