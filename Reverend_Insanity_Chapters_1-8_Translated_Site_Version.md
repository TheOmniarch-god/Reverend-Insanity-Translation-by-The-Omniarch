## Preface
# Preface: Either Toward Success or Toward Destruction

In the past few months I have lived through pain in both body and mind, and I have been struggling with it.

Life is like a balance scale, with reality on one end and the inner self on the other. For me the reality side grew heavier and heavier until the scale tipped, and that is where the pain came from.

People walk a road for so long that they lose themselves in the scenery along the way and often forget why they set out in the first place.

That is what happened. In the midst of that pain I felt waves of bewilderment.

Since my last book was completed, I have been on a break, trying to find my direction and recover the reason I set out in the first place.

That was about five or six years ago. I had already been reading webnovels for a while. The first was A Journey to the Ethereal, then Blasphemy, Zhu Xian, Mad God, House of Beast Tamers, Boiling Beast Blood, Mount Shu, I Alone Am Immortal, and so on.

I had read a great many novels, but a knot of frustration built up in my chest. The protagonists I saw were almost always blessed with heaven defying fortune and were upright and noble in character. The villains were mostly foolish, deranged and ugly, their titles resounding, yet they were all paper tigers, impressive to look at until they met the protagonist, and then they wilted.

So I began to think that I wanted to see a truly great villain.

This villain would be uncompromising and solitary, proud, dark and decisive in killing, never weak in the knees. He would never hide his own evil, and he would have no false goodness at all.

At a glance you could tell he was utterly evil, his demonic aura stark and his killing intent filling the air. What he was best at was trampling rules and slaughtering the famed righteous sects.

Alone, he would be an enemy to the whole world, sowing terror among all living things. He would be a great master of the demonic path, sometimes arrogant and domineering, sometimes sinister and cunning, sometimes cold and merciless, standing proudly at the summit and looking down on the world with disdain, while every brave soul who dared to challenge him came to a miserable end.

He was cruel in his methods and perhaps even dabbled with Gu worms. In short, you could tell at a glance that he was no decent person at all.

So what should his name be... Super Demon King? Black Mountain Old Demon? Green Robe Ancestor?

No, no, no.

After thinking it over, I thought, why not call him Gu Zhen Ren?

It just so happened that I needed to create an account on Qidian at the time, so I thought, okay, and that ID was born.

Some TV dramas always do it like this: the villain defeats the protagonist and is just one final lethal strike away from killing him. Then the villain starts rambling on and on, while the protagonist secretly gathers his strength for one last gamble, and every time he turns the tables and wins a complete victory.

If the situation is reversed and the villain is the one at a disadvantage, it goes like this. The protagonist unleashes his power and the villain is beaten to the ground. Facing the protagonist's sword, the villain suddenly bursts into tears, wailing and swearing to mend his ways. The protagonist, who is kindhearted and benevolent, hesitates. At that moment the villain launches a desperate counterattack, but it fails. The protagonist, who sees every detail with perfect clarity, completely subdues him. His heart hardens at the same time, he shouts, Despicable, shameless, vile, and finally kills the villain.

There are also villains who kidnap the protagonist's family and friends, and almost never succeed in using them as leverage. Even with a huge advantage, they somehow get defeated by the protagonist for all sorts of unavoidable reasons and in baffling ways.

It was about six years ago that I created the ID Gu Zhen Ren, because I had a wish in my heart to see a truly evil archvillain. He would have his own way of doing things, tolerated by no one in this world, roaming free and unrestrained and disdaining any explanation. He would be decisive in killing, never trapped by feelings, and never weak in the knees even at the sight of a beautiful woman.

His dreaded name made the whole world tremble, and at the mere mention of him people paled.

Those who understood him admired him, revered him and loved him. Those who hated him gritted their teeth and used every means they had, only to make him much stronger.

He walked his own path, solitude wrapped around him like a cloak, and his cold eyes could see through every falsehood.

When faced with criticism and rejection, he scoffed with disdain. When faced with hollow praise and flattery, he sneered in silence.

He could endure solitude. He could even enjoy it.

If he was not walking toward success, then he was walking toward destruction.

He was a warrior, making a lone pilgrimage through darkness, cutting through thorns and brambles all the way. No matter what stood in his way, nothing could stop him. If a god stood in his way, he would kill the god. If a Buddha stood in his way, he would kill the Buddha!

This dream had always been buried deep in my heart. During these years of writing, I thought several times about starting to write it, but nothing came of it in the end. I did not quite dare. I felt my skill was not yet good enough.

And so, so many years passed. I went through some things, and compared with when I first started writing, much of it now is a case of things remaining but people having changed.

The past was somewhat unbearable to look back on, and this dream was also hidden away in the dust of time, gradually forgotten by me. Even when I did remember it now and then, I felt I was not prepared enough and still lacked the ability to write such a cathartic story.

But these past few days I stopped walking forward and looked back, and I suddenly understood something. Life is not cooking. When you cook, you usually have the oil, salt, soy sauce and vinegar prepared beforehand, and only then do you start.

Life does not wait for you to be ready. Even when you think everything is ready, do you not still lack a wisp of east wind?

Life is always rushed. Many things happen suddenly with little to no preparation.

Fate is unpredictable and always changing. No one can tell what will happen tomorrow.

So I made a decision. Against the backdrop of the prophesied end of the world in 2012 drawing near, I would write a new book.

This book would be called Gu Zhen Ren.

No other title could suit it better.

This is a cause planted six years ago, bearing fruit in the present.

Giving the book the same name has no other meaning. It is not narcissism or arrogance, just a way to fulfill a dream. To fulfill that original dream and to recover that feeling that once moved me.

There is no doubt that this is a book that will invite controversy. In the words of the LongKong crowd, this is what you would call having warped values.

Yes, I will say it plainly here. This is a book with warped values.

Because the protagonist of this book is a great villain who will stop at nothing to achieve his ends and never gives up, supremely evil. While the villains in this book are mostly great, glorious and upright young heroes, or reputable and honorable orthodox sects.

I know this will disappoint many people, but please try to understand. Those who abandon their principles the moment they see a beautiful woman, do they still have the bearing of a peerless demon king or a great boss of the demonic path?

That is just being lovesick.

That is right. This book will kill women, and beautiful women at that. It will kill them in great numbers and without restraint.

And it will never hold back. No matter what kind of existence it is, as long as it is an enemy. No matter how powerful he is, it will fight him. No matter how beautiful she is, it will kill her all the same.

This book is very evil. Read with caution.

Reading is, after all, a hobby, something done for pleasure.

You read to have fun. If you are truly not happy, if you do not enjoy it, then do not read it. Just switch to another book. There are a great many good books on Qidian, and I will recommend one or two as I write.

If you cannot continue, if you feel unhappy, if you feel your outlook on life, your values and your morals are being trampled, then you have my respectful send off.

Those who want to read it are naturally welcome.

The above is the preface to this book, and also a declaration.

## Chapter 1
# Chapter 1: Though the Flesh Perish, the Demonic Heart Knows No Regret

“Fang Yuan! Hand over the Spring Autumn Cicada without struggle, and I shall grant you a quick death!”

“Old Demon Fang, harbor no delusions of defiance! Today, the revered sects of the Righteous Path have united for one purpose: to trample your demonic lair into dust. We have long since laid an inescapable dragnet here. This time, you shall be beheaded!”

“Fang Yuan, you damned devil! To refine that cursed Spring Autumn Cicada, you slaughtered tens of millions of innocent lives! You have committed towering atrocities, unpardonable, unredeemable, innumerable, beyond all count!”

“Monster! Three hundred years ago, you violated me and robbed me of my chastity. You killed my entire family and slaughtered all my kin. From that moment on, I’ve wanted nothing more than to devour your flesh and drink your blood! Today, I’ll make you wish you were dead!”

* * *

Fang Yuan stood cloaked in a tattered emerald robe, his hair wild and unkempt, his body bathed in blood, as his eyes swept over the surrounding host. The mountain wind whipped his blood-soaked garments, fluttering and snapping like a war banner. Fresh blood gushed out from the hundreds of wounds across his body. In the mere moment he had stood there, a large pool of blood had already gathered at his feet. Besieged on every flank by bitter foes, every path to life had long been severed. The trap had snapped shut; on this day, death was absolute.

Fang Yuan perceived his doom with crystalline clarity. Yet even as the shadow of death loomed before him, he remained expressionless, his demeanor unmoved. His eyes were abyssal, like an ancient well, unfathomably deep, without shore and without bottom. The righteous host besieging him comprised distinguished sect leaders and young heroes renowned far and wide. Yet as they surrounded Fang Yuan, their composure fractured: some bared their teeth in savage roars, some sneered with brittle arrogance, some narrowed their eyes with venomous suspicion, while others clutched bleeding wounds, looking upon him with lingering dread.

None of them dared make a move; every soul among them trembled before the final, dying wrath of Old Demon Fang. In this suffocating deadlock, six hours slipped into eternity. The sun sank beneath the western ridge; its dying embers set the clouds ablaze, bathing the mountain crest in a fierce, incandescent conflagration of twilight. Fang Yuan, who had stood as motionless as a statue, slowly turned around. That solitary motion sent a convulsion through the host; the multitude recoiled in unison, retreating a full pace in panic. At this moment, the grey-white mountain rocks beneath Fang Yuan’s feet had long been dyed dark red with fresh blood. His face, pale from excessive blood loss, was reflected by the sunset glow, suddenly taking on an alluring, delicate radiance.

Gazing upon the green mountains and the setting sun, Fang Yuan let slip a soft, low chuckle:

“Green mountains beneath the setting sun, autumn moon and spring breeze.

Truly, in the morning one’s hair is like black silk, by dusk it turns to snow; Right and wrong, success and failure, all vanish in the turning of a head.” As the verse left his lips, memories of his past life on Earth surged unbidden before his eyes. He had originally been a Chinese student on Earth, cast into this foreign world through a serendipitous twist of fortune. Three hundred years of drifting, tossed like flotsam upon tempestuous tides; two hundred years of unbridled dominion, carving his will across the world, more than five centuries, yet in the retrospect of memory, gone in the span of an eyeblink.

Countless memories buried deep within the bedrock of his soul awakened, playing across his mind with breathtaking, indelible clarity.

“In the end, I failed after all,” Fang Yuan sighed in his heart. A trace of wistfulness stirred, yet not the faintest whisper of regret.

This outcome was nothing he had not foreseen; the moment he made his choice centuries ago, he was already mentally prepared. For what was the Demonic Path? It was to cultivate no virtuous karma, committing murder and arson; spurned by heaven and reviled by earth, making enemies with the whole world, and still running wild, following the whim of one’s own heart to the very end.

“If this newly refined Spring Autumn Cicada works, I will still be a demon in my next life!”

Thinking like this, Fang Yuan could not help laughing out loud.

“Old Demon, what are you laughing at?!”

“Everyone be careful, the demon is at death’s door and wants to counterattack!”

“Quickly hand over the Spring Autumn Cicada!!”

The righteous host surged forward, greed and panic driving them into frenzy. And in that very breath. With a thunderous blast, Fang Yuan brazenly self-detonated.

* * *

Spring rain fell continuously, silently nourishing Qing Mao Mountain. Night was already deep, and threads of cool wind blew through the fine rain. Qing Mao Mountain was not dark, however; from the mountainside down to the foothills, faint, shimmering lights glowed, as if draping a brilliant ribbon of radiance across the slope. These lights came from the stilt houses, not ten thousand households, but several thousand nonetheless. This was Gu Yue Village, nestled upon Qing Mao Mountain, giving the vast, tranquil mountain range a distinct sense of human habitation. At the very heart of Gu Yue Village stood a grand and glorious pavilion. Right now a grand sacrificial ceremony was being held, so it was brightly lit and brilliantly glorious.

“Ancestors bless us! Hope that in this Aperture Opening Ceremony, many youths with excellent aptitude can emerge to bring fresh blood and hope to our clan!”

The Gu Yue Clan Leader appeared middle-aged, his temples touched with frost. Wearing plain white, solemn sacrificial robes, he knelt upon the brownish-yellow floorboards, his upper body erect, hands pressed together, closing both eyes to pray sincerely. He faced a tall, black-lacquered altar table with three tiers, enshrining the spirit tablets of the ancestors. On both sides of the tablets stood red-copper incense burners, thin plumes of fragrant smoke coiling upward. Behind him, ten-odd figures knelt in matching white sacrificial robes. These were the clan elders and decision-makers, each wielding power over various affairs of the clan.

After concluding his prayers, the Clan Leader was the first to bend down, palms flat against the wooden floorboards, kowtowing. His forehead met the brown floor with soft, rhythmic thuds. The elders behind him, their expressions solemn, followed suit in silence. For a time, the ancestral hall was filled only with the gentle cadence of foreheads knocking against the floor. When the ceremony concluded, everyone slowly rose from the floor and quietly filed out of the solemn hall. In the corridor, the elders breathed a quiet sigh of relief, the tense atmosphere easing noticeably.

Murmurs of conversation soon began to rise:

“Time truly flies. In the blink of an eye, another full year has gone by.”

“The previous Aperture Opening Ceremony feels like it happened just yesterday; the memory is still vivid.”

“Tomorrow is the annual Aperture Opening Ceremony. I wonder what kind of fresh blood our clan will produce this year?”

“Sigh, I only hope a youth with Grade A aptitude emerges. It has been three years since our Gu Yue clan had such a genius.”

“Indeed. Both Bai Village and Xiong Village have seen remarkable talents emerge over recent years. In particular, Bai Ning Bing of the Bai clan, his natural talent is truly terrifying.”

Whoever mentioned the name Bai Ning Bing, a shadow of worry involuntarily crossed the faces of the clan elders. This youth’s talent was extraordinary. In just two short years, he had already cultivated to Rank 3 Gu Master, standing unrivaled among the younger generation. Even the older generation felt immense pressure from this rising star. Given time, he would undoubtedly become the pillar of Bai Village, at least a powerhouse standing on his own. No one ever doubted this.

“However, among the youths participating in this year’s Aperture Opening Ceremony, it is not as though we have no hope.”

“Quite right. A genius youth has appeared in the Fang lineage. He could speak at three months and walk at four. By age five, he could compose poems and recite verses, exceptionally intelligent and overflowing with talent. It is just a pity his parents died early, and he is currently being raised by his aunt and uncle.”

“Mm, he possesses precocious wisdom and grand ambitions. In recent years, I have heard of the poems he composed, Bring in the Wine, Ode to the Plum Blossom, and River Town. He is truly a genius!”

The Gu Yue Clan Leader was the last to exit the ancestral hall. As he slowly closed the door, the murmurs of the elders reached his ears. He immediately knew that the elders were discussing a youth named Gu Yue Fang Yuan. As the leader of the clan, he naturally paid close attention to outstanding juniors, and Gu Yue Fang Yuan was the most prominent and dazzling among them. Experience had shown that those gifted with photographic memories or strength rivaling an adult from childhood almost always possessed superior cultivation talent.

“If this child tests with Grade A aptitude and is properly nurtured, he may well be able to contend with Bai Ning Bing. Even if he only has Grade B aptitude, he will certainly become a stalwart pillar and a banner for the Gu Yue clan in the future. Yet with such precocious intellect, Grade B is unlikely, it is overwhelmingly probable that he has Grade A.”

With this thought, the corners of the Clan Leader’s lips lifted slightly into a smile. He cleared his throat with a dry cough and addressed the elders: “Everyone, the hour is late. For the sake of tomorrow’s Aperture Opening Ceremony, tonight please be sure to rest well and conserve your energy.” Hearing this, the elders paused slightly. In the glances they exchanged, a subtle trace of vigilance was concealed. The Clan Leader’s words were polite and restrained, but everyone understood their deeper meaning. Every year, in order to recruit talented juniors into their own factions, the elders fought tooth and nail until their faces flushed red and heads bled.

It was indeed time to rest and gather strength, so they could contend fiercely tomorrow. Especially for that Gu Yue Fang Yuan, the probability of him possessing Grade A aptitude was exceptionally high. Moreover, both his parents were deceased, leaving him and his brother as the only surviving orphans of the Fang lineage. If one could bring him into their own faction and groom him well, it could guarantee a century of prosperity for their line!

“However, let me make the ugly words clear first. Compete openly and honorably; underhanded schemes that harm clan unity will not be tolerated. Elders, bear this firmly in mind!” the Clan Leader warned sternly.

“We wouldn’t dare, we wouldn’t dare.”

“We shall definitely keep it firmly in mind.”

“Then we shall take our leave. Lord Clan Leader, please do not trouble yourself to see us off.”

Harboring their own calculations, the elders dispersed one by one. Before long, the long corridor grew quiet and desolate. Spring rain and slanted wind blew through the window; the Clan Leader stepped lightly toward the window frame. Instantly, a mouthful of fresh, moist mountain air filled his chest, refreshing to the soul. This was the third floor of the attic; the Clan Leader looked out, and more than half of Gu Yue Village could be seen in a single glance. Though it was late at night, lights still burned in most homes in the village, quite unlike usual.

Tomorrow was the Aperture Opening Ceremony, touching upon everyone’s personal interests. An excited and nervous atmosphere covered the clansmen’s hearts, naturally making many sleep restlessly.

“This is the future hope of our clan,” with dots of light reflected in his eyes, the Clan Leader sighed softly.

* * *

At this very moment, another pair of clear eyes quietly gazed upon those twinkling lights in the deep of the night, brimming with complex emotions.

“Gu Yue Village… this is five hundred years ago?! The Spring Autumn Cicada really had effect…”

Fang Yuan’s gaze was dark and profound as he stood beside the window, letting the wind and rain beat against his body. The effect of the Spring Autumn Cicada was to reverse time. Ranked seventh among the Ten Mystical Gu, it was naturally no ordinary existence.

In short: rebirth.

“Reborn using the Spring Autumn Cicada… back to five hundred years ago!”

Fang Yuan reached out his hand, staring intently at his young, tender, and somewhat pale palm. Then he slowly clenched it into a fist, feeling the vivid reality with all his strength. He heard the gentle pitter-patter of drizzle tapping against the window lattice. He closed his eyes slowly, opening them only after a long moment, letting out a wistful sigh:

“Five hundred years of experience… it truly feels like a dream.”

Yet he knew with absolute certainty: this was no dream.

## Chapter 2
In legends, there exists a River of Time in this world, supporting the flow of all existence. And by utilizing the power of the Spring Autumn Cicada, one can travel upstream against the current, returning to the past. Regarding this rumor, the world was divided in opinion. Many did not believe it, while some half-believed and half-doubted. Almost no one was truly certain. Because every time the Spring Autumn Cicada was used, one had to pay with their life, sacrificing their entire body and all of their cultivation base as the driving power.

This price was truly too steep, and what made it even harder to accept was that even after paying with one’s life, the outcome remained unknown. Even if someone obtained the Spring Autumn Cicada, they would never dare use it casually on a whim. What if the rumor was false, nothing more than a hoax? If Fang Yuan had not been driven to a dead end, he would not have used it so readily. However, now, Fang Yuan believed completely and thoroughly. Because the ironclad truth lay before his eyes, beyond all dispute. He had indeed been reborn!

“It is only a pity for such a good Gu. Back then, it took monumental effort, the slaughter of hundreds of thousands of people, stirring the wrath of heaven and the resentment of men, enduring endless tribulations before I finally refined it…” Fang Yuan sighed inwardly. Though he had been reborn, the Spring Autumn Cicada had not traveled back with him.

Humans are the spirit of all living things; Gu are the essence of heaven and earth. Gu were bizarre and varied, countless in number. Some Gu would completely dissipate after being used once or twice. Other Gu, as long as they were not used excessively, could be used repeatedly. Perhaps the Spring Autumn Cicada was simply that kind of expendable Gu that could only be used once.

“However, even if it is gone, I can simply refine another one. If I could refine it in my past life, could I not in this life?” Pity aside, a surge of ambitious pride could not help rising in Fang Yuan’s heart.

To be able to be reborn, the loss of the Spring Autumn Cicada was entirely acceptable. Moreover, he still carried a priceless treasure; he was far from empty-handed. This treasure was his five hundred years of memories and experience. Within his memory lay countless secret troves, currently unopened by anyone. There lay major historical events one by one, allowing him to easily grasp the pulse of history. There lay countless figures: some were hidden experts who cultivate in deep seclusion, some were extraordinary prodigies, and some were not even born yet. There also lay the grueling experience of bitter cultivation over these five hundred years, and a rich reservoir of battle experience.

With these, he undoubtedly held the overall situation and the initiative. As long as he played his cards well, roaming the mortal realm and recreating his former glory as a demonic overlord and ruthless hegemon would be no problem at all, and he could even take a step further, charging towards an even higher realm!

“Then how should I proceed…?” Fang Yuan was extremely rational. He quickly gathered his emotions and turned toward the night rain outside the window, falling into deep thought.

Thinking like this, he felt a thousand loose ends and ten thousand tangled threads. After pondering for a moment, his brow furrowed deeper and deeper. A span of five hundred years was truly rather long. Not to mention those memories that were already blurred and could no longer be recalled, even among the secret treasure grounds and immortal opportunities he did remember, although numerous, most were either separated by hundreds of thousands of miles, or required specific times to open.

“The most crucial factor is still cultivation base. Right now, my primeval sea is not yet open; I have not even stepped onto the cultivation path of a Gu Master. I am fundamentally a mortal! I must cultivate as quickly as possible and raise my cultivation base, moving ahead of history to seize the initiative as much as possible and grab enough benefits.”

Moreover, without sufficient cultivation, he could not make full use of many hidden troves even if he obtained them. Instead, they would become dangerous liabilities, merely possessing such treasures would make him a target. The first difficult problem placed before Fang Yuan was his cultivation base. He had to raise his cultivation base as quickly as possible; if he was as slow as in his past life, even the daylilies would have gone cold.

“To raise my cultivation base quickly, I must rely on the resources of the clan. In my current situation, I fundamentally lack the capability to traverse the peril-laden mountain ranges; even an ordinary wild boar could take my life. Only if I reach the cultivation base of a Rank 3 Gu Master will I have the baseline ability to protect myself and traverse the mountains and rivers of this world.”

Through the eyes of a demonic overlord tempered by five hundred years, this Qing Mao Mountain was truly too small, and Gu Yue Village was more like a cage. However, while a cage imprisons freedom, a sturdy cell often represents a kind of safety.

“Hmph. For now, I’ll make my moves within this cage. As long as I advance to Rank 3 Gu Master, I will leave this impoverished mountain backwater. Fortunately, tomorrow is the Aperture Opening Ceremony, and shortly after, I can officially begin cultivating as a Gu Master.”

At the thought of the Aperture Opening Ceremony, memories long buried in dust surfaced within Fang Yuan’s heart.

“Aptitude, is it…?” Looking outside the window, he could not help letting out three cold chuckles.

Just at this moment, the room door was gently pushed open, and a youth walked in.

“Brother, why are you standing by the window letting the rain fall on you?”

The youth was thin in build, slightly shorter than Fang Yuan, his face bearing an uncanny resemblance to his own. Fang Yuan turned his head to look at this youth, a trace of complexity flashing across his face.

“So it is you, my twin brother.” He raised his brows slightly, his expression returning to his habitual coldness.

Fang Zheng lowered his head, looking down at his toes, his trademark habit. “Seeing that brother’s window wasn’t closed, I wanted to slip in quietly and shut it. Tomorrow is the Aperture Opening Ceremony. Brother, if Uncle and Aunt find out you’re still up this late, I’m afraid they’ll worry.” He was not surprised by Fang Yuan’s coldness, because from childhood to now, his brother had always been like this. Sometimes he would think that perhaps geniuses were just different from ordinary mortals. Although he shared a nearly identical face with his brother, he felt as ordinary as an ant.

Born from the same womb at the same time, why was heaven so unfair? It had given his brother diamond-like talent, while he was ordinary like a roadside stone. Everyone around him, whenever mentioning him, would always say: “This is Fang Yuan’s brother.” His uncle and aunt also often lectured him: “You must learn from your brother.” Sometimes, even looking at his own face in the mirror filled him with a certain disgust! These thoughts had existed for many years, accumulating deep inside his heart day by day. They weighed on his chest like a boulder. Over the years, Fang Zheng’s head had hung lower and lower, and he had grown increasingly silent.

“Worry…?” Thinking of his uncle and aunt, Fang Yuan scoffed inwardly.

He remembered very clearly that the parents of this body had both fallen during a clan mission. When he was merely three years old, he and his brother had become orphans. Under the pretext of raising them, his uncle and aunt had openly and legally usurped their parents’ inheritance, and treated the two brothers harshly. Originally, as a transmigrator, he had planned to bide his time in obscurity. But the hardship of survival had forced Fang Yuan to choose displaying talent different from normal people. The so-called “genius” was actually nothing more than the rationality of a mature soul, paired with a few Tang poems and Song verses from Earth that echoed through generations.

Just testing his hands like this, he was amazed at as a heavenly being, receiving widespread attention. Under external pressure, it also made the young Fang Yuan have to choose a cold expression to disguise and protect himself, reducing the chance of exposing his secrets. Over time, coldness instead became his habitual expression. Just like this, his uncle and aunt could no longer treat him and his brother harshly. As his age grew and his future looked brighter, his treatment also increased along with it. However, this was not love, but an investment.

The laughable thing was that this brother of his had never seen through this truth. Not only was he blinded by their uncle and aunt, but he had even harbored a buried resentment toward Fang Yuan. Don’t look at him being so well-behaved and honest now; in Fang Yuan’s memories, once he tested with Grade A aptitude and was heavily nurtured by the clan, all of his hidden hatred and jealousy had been released, and he had spared no effort in targeting, making things difficult for, and suppressing his own elder brother.

And as for his own aptitude…? Heh. At best, merely of Grade C. Fate always loved to play jokes. Born as twins, the elder brother’s aptitude was only of Grade C, yet monopolized the name of genius for over a decade. The younger brother had been unknown, yet possessed Grade A natural talent. The result of the Aperture Opening Ceremony had left the entire clan stunned. It also completely reversed the two brothers’ situation and treatment. The younger brother was like a crouching dragon ascending the heavens; the elder brother was like a fledgling phoenix crashing to the earth.

After that, came repeated difficulties from his brother, cold eyes from his uncle and aunt, and contempt from the clansmen.

Did he hate them?

In his past life, Fang Yuan had hated. He hated his own insufficient aptitude, hated the clan’s heartlessness, and hated fate’s unfairness. But now, as he reexamined that part of his life with five hundred years of experience behind him, he felt not a ripple of hatred.

What was there to resent?

Thinking from their perspective, he could also understand his brother, his uncle and aunt, as well as those righteous powerhouses who besieged him five hundred years later. The strong devour the weak; survival of the fittest, that was the very nature of this world. Moreover, everyone has their own ambitions, all fighting for that sliver of heavenly opportunity. Between each other suppressing and killing, what was there not to understand? Five hundred years of experience had long made him see through all of this; in his heart, there remained only the Great Dao of Eternal Life.

If someone stood in the way of this pursuit, no matter who, it was nothing more than: either you die or I live. The wild ambition in his heart was too immense. Stepping onto this path, one was destined to make enemies of the entire world, destined to walk alone, and destined to face relentless tribulations. This was the enlightenment distilled from five hundred years of life.

“Vengeance is not my intention, and the demonic path also never has the word compromise.” Thinking to this point, Fang Yuan could not help laughing. Turning his head toward this brother of his, taking a faint glance, he said: “You can go.”

Fang Zheng felt a sudden tremor in his heart, feeling that his brother’s gaze was as sharp as an icy blade, seeming to pierce into the deepest part of his heart. Under that gaze, he felt as if he were standing naked in the snow, every secret laid bare.

“Then… see you tomorrow, brother.” Daring not speak more right now, Fang Zheng slowly closed the room door, murmuring compliance as he retreated.

## Chapter 3
Clack, clack-clack. Clack, clack-clack. The night watchman was making his rounds, striking his wooden clapper in a steady rhythm. As the hollow strikes drifted into the stilt house, Fang Yuan opened his dry, heavy eyelids, murmuring inwardly: “It is the fifth watch.” Yesternight, he had lain in bed pondering long into the night, making plans and arrangements. By his reckoning, he had slept for barely a little over two hours. This body had not yet begun cultivation and had little stamina; he still felt tired, and sleep kept tugging at his body and mind.

Yet five hundred years of trials had long tempered Fang Yuan’s willpower into profound, unyielding steel. A faint trace of lethargy meant nothing to him. Without hesitation, he tossed aside the thin silk quilt and rose to his feet with crisp precision. Pushing open the wooden window, he saw that the spring rain had ceased. Fresh, moist air carrying the scents of earth, trees, and wildflowers rushed into his face. Fang Yuan immediately felt his mind clear, his drowsiness swept away completely. The sun had not yet crested the horizon; the sky was a deep, brooding azure, caught between shadow and dawn. Looking out across the expanse, stilt dwellings constructed of green bamboo and timber blended seamlessly against the surrounding mountains, painting a tranquil panorama of verdant emerald.

These stilt houses were built at least two stories high, an architectural hallmark of the mountain folk. Because the rugged terrain was steep and uneven, the ground level consisted of massive timber stilts, while the upper floor served as living quarters. Fang Yuan and his younger brother, Fang Zheng, lived on this second floor.

Fang Yuan glanced down to see his personal maidservant, Shen Cui. Her looks were merely average, but she dressed with care, wearing a green tunic with long sleeves and matching trousers, embroidered shoes on her feet, and a single pearl hairpin nestled in her dark hair, radiating youthful vitality from head to toe. She cast a delighted glance up at Fang Yuan, then hurried up the wooden stairs with a basin of water, her brisk footsteps pattering against the planks. The water had been prepared to a gentle warmth for washing his face. To rinse his mouth, a willow twig dipped in fine snow-salt was used to clean and whiten the teeth.

Shen Cui attended him with gentle care, a bright smile on her face and coquettish warmth in her eyes. As she helped Fang Yuan dress and fasten his clasps, she brushed her plump bosom against his arm and back from time to time. Fang Yuan remained expressionless, his heart like still water. This maidservant was not only his aunt and uncle’s informant, but she was also vain and fickle. In his past life, he had once been blinded by her charms; yet after the Aperture Opening Ceremony, when his status plummeted overnight, she instantly turned against him, giving him no shortage of cold disdain.

When Fang Zheng came in, he saw Shen Cui smoothing the creases in Fang Yuan’s clothes over his chest, and a glint of envy and jealousy involuntarily flickered in his eyes. Living with his elder brother all these years and benefiting from Fang Yuan’s care, he too had a servant to attend him. Yet it was not a young maid like Shen Cui, but a fat, bloated old woman.

“If Shen Cui could attend to me like this one day, how would that feel?” Fang Zheng thought about it somewhat, yet dared not think about it fully.

That their aunt and uncle favored Fang Yuan was common knowledge throughout the household. Originally, he had no servant to attend him; it was actually Fang Yuan who took the initiative to demand one for him. Although Fang Zheng was the master and Shen Cui the servant, he ordinarily did not dare look down on her. Her mother was Granny Shen, who attended their aunt and also managed the entire household. Deeply trusted by their aunt, she wielded considerable authority.

“Enough. There’s no need to fuss anymore.” Fang Yuan impatiently brushed aside Shen Cui’s soft little hands. His clothes had long been smooth; she was doing more to entice him than to straighten them.

To her, Fang Yuan had a bright future and was very likely to possess Grade A aptitude. If she could become his concubine, she could rise from servant to mistress, a single leap to the heavens. In his previous life, Fang Yuan had been deceived, even falling in love with this maidservant. Now, reborn, he saw through her completely, his heart as cold as frost.

“You can go.” Without so much as looking at Shen Cui, Fang Yuan adjusted his cuffs.

Shen Cui pouted slightly, feeling puzzled and a little hurt by his indifference to her advances today. She wanted to say something coquettish, but an inexplicable air about Fang Yuan, barely perceptible, intimidated her. She opened her mouth several times, but in the end merely said, “Yes,” and obediently left.

“Are you ready?” Fang Yuan looked toward Fang Zheng.

His younger brother stood blankly at the doorway. Lowering his head to look at his toes, he gave a quiet murmur of assent. He had actually woken during the fourth watch. Too nervous to sleep, he had slipped out of bed and prepared himself early. There were dark circles under both his eyes. Fang Yuan nodded. In his previous life, he had not understood what was on his younger brother’s mind. But in this life, how could he fail to understand? There was no point in exposing it now, however. He simply said, “Then let’s go.”

The two brothers left their residence. Along the way, they encountered quite a few youths their own age, in twos and threes, evidently heading to the same destination.

“Look, those are the two Fang brothers.” Cautious whispers reached their ears.

“The one walking in front is Fang Yuan, the Fang Yuan who writes poetry,” someone emphasized.

“So that’s him. Expressionless, acting as though no one else exists, he really is as arrogant as the rumors say.” The speaker’s sour tone carried both envy and jealousy.

“Hmph. If you could do what he does, you could act that arrogant too!” someone answered with a cold snort, concealing a note of discontent.

Fang Zheng listened expressionlessly. He had long grown accustomed to such talk. Head lowered, he followed silently behind his elder brother. By now, the horizon glowed with the light of dawn, and Fang Yuan’s shadow fell across his face. The morning sun was gradually rising, yet Fang Zheng suddenly felt that he was walking into darkness. This darkness came from his elder brother. Perhaps, for his entire life, he would never escape the enormous shadow his brother cast over him. Tightness gripped his chest again and again, until even breathing became difficult. This damned feeling brought one word to mind: suffocation!

“Hmph. Such talk. Truly, the tree that rises above the forest bears the brunt of the wind.” Listening to the comments around him, Fang Yuan sneered inwardly.

No wonder that, after his aptitude had been tested as Grade C, he had found himself surrounded by enemies, subjected to harsh treatment, cold disdain, and neglect for a long time. He also caught every sound of his younger brother’s increasingly labored breathing behind him. What he had failed to notice in his previous life, he now perceived down to the smallest detail. This keen insight came entirely from five hundred years of life experience. He suddenly thought of his uncle and aunt. They certainly had their methods. They had assigned Shen Cui to monitor him at close quarters, while giving his younger brother an old woman. There were other differences in their treatment, too, in the details of daily life. All of this was deliberate, intended to stir his younger brother’s sense of injustice and drive a wedge between them.

People worry not about having little, but about having unequal shares. In his previous life, he had been too inexperienced, and his younger brother too foolish and naive. Their uncle and aunt had succeeded in setting them against each other. Now that he had been reborn, the Aperture Opening Ceremony was already at hand. The situation seemed too deeply entrenched to reverse, but with Fang Yuan’s methods and wisdom as a demonic overlord, it could still be changed. This younger brother of his could certainly be subdued and brought to heel. Shen Cui, a mere slip of a maid, could all the more easily be taken into his harem ahead of time. As for his uncle and aunt, the clan leader, and the clan elders, he had at least several hundred ways to put them in their place.

So what if he was his own younger brother? Without familial affection to speak of, he was merely an outsider. Discard him, and so be it. Even if Shen Cui were even more beautiful, so what? Devoid of love and loyalty, she is nothing more than flesh. Her, in my harem? She’s not worthy. So what if they were his uncle and aunt, the clan leader, or the clan elders? They were all merely passing through his life. Why exhaust his ingenuity and waste his energy putting these passersby in their place?

He chuckled.

As long as they do not block my path, they can piss off. I wouldn’t even deign to step on them.

## Chapter 4
The morning sun rose, the clouds aglow with brilliant light. The mountain mist was thin, and sunlight pierced it easily like a sharp sword. More than a hundred fifteen-year-old youths were now gathered before the Clan Leader’s Pavilion. The Clan Leader’s Pavilion stood at the very heart of the village, five stories high, with projected eaves and upturned corners. It was heavily guarded. A square lay before it, and inside were enshrined the spirit tablets of the Gu Yue ancestors. Each successive clan leader also lived there. Whenever an important ceremony or a major unforeseen event arose, the clan elders would be convened there to deliberate. It was the center of power for the entire village.

His hair and beard were white, but he remained full of vigor as he led the youths into the Clan Leader’s Pavilion.

The youths exclaimed in wonder. The underground cavern was magnificent. Standing amid the crowd, Fang Yuan quietly examined everything, thinking inwardly: “Several hundred years ago, the Gu Yue clan migrated from Central Continent to Southern Border and settled on Qing Mao Mountain. In the Gu Yue clan, the proportion was somewhat higher: six out of ten. This was because the Gu Yue ancestor, the first clan leader, had been a renowned, legendary expert. His cultivation had caused genes carrying power to lie hidden within his bloodline. As his descendants, members of the Gu Yue clan generally possessed higher aptitude.

He reached thirty-six steps before he could go no farther. The youths across the river stared, their eyes wide and mouths agape. The Academy Elder cried out in delight, “Excellent! Gu Yue Mo Bei, Grade B aptitude! Come here and let me examine your primeval sea.” Gu Yue Mo Bei returned to the Academy Elder’s side. Primeval sea: sixty-six percent. Worth substantial investment in his development.”

After two years of training, most youths with Grade C aptitude could become Rank 2 Gu Masters, forming the backbone of the clan. A youth with Grade B aptitude warranted special care. In other words, provided Gu Yue Mo Bei fulfilled his potential, he would become an elder of the Gu Yue clan. No wonder the Academy Elder laughed in delight. The elders observing discreetly all breathed a sigh of relief, then turned to one of their colleagues with envy.

My grandson isn’t bad, is he?” The red-haired elder, Gu Yue Chi Lian, gave a cold snort but made no reply, his expression sullen and grim. An hour later, half the youths had entered the flower field. Quite a few had shown Grade C or Grade D aptitude, though nearly half of those tested had none at all.

The fourth clan leader was our only Rank 5 expert, but he perished alongside the Flower Wine Monk and left no descendants. The aptitude of our younger generations keeps declining.” The clan leader sighed deeply.

Gu Yue Chi Cheng was short and slight, his face covered in pockmarks. With his fists clenched and sweat covering his face, he looked especially nervous. He stepped onto the opposite bank, and points of light entered his body one after another. He continued for thirty-six steps before stopping.

“Another Grade B!” the Academy Elder exclaimed.

A stir ran through the youths as they turned envious eyes toward Gu Yue Chi Cheng.

“Thirty-six steps! Thirty-six!” Gu Yue Chi Lian shouted with a triumphant laugh, glaring challengingly at Gu Yue Mo Chen.

This time, it was Gu Yue Mo Chen’s turn to look livid.

His aptitude was actually only Grade C, but his grandfather, Gu Yue Chi Lian, had arranged the deception that made it appear to be Grade B. If Fang Yuan wanted to cheat, he had dozens of methods in mind, some more sophisticated than the one used for Gu Yue Chi Cheng. If he appeared to possess Grade B or even Grade A aptitude, the clan would certainly devote substantial resources to his development.

Secondly, even if he succeeded, he could not conceal his subsequent cultivation speed. The deception would still come to light. Gu Yue Chi Cheng, however, was different. His grandfather, Gu Yue Chi Lian, was one of the clan’s two most powerful elders and could cover for him.

A sharp gleam flickered in Fang Yuan’s eyes as he considered how to exploit this knowledge for the greatest possible benefit. Exposing the deception now would earn him a small reward from the clan, but it would also antagonize the influential, high-ranking Gu Yue Chi Lian. That was out of the question. Blackmail was no option for now, either. He was too weak; attempting it would only bring disaster upon himself. As he considered this, he suddenly heard the Academy Elder call his name:

“Gu Yue Fang Yuan!”

## Chapter 5
His surroundings fell quiet in an instant. Countless eyes turned toward him.

“This is getting more and more interesting.” Fang Yuan smiled inwardly. Under everyone’s watchful eyes, he waded across the river and stepped onto the opposite bank.

Immediately, he felt a layer of pressure bearing against him. The pressure came from the spirit spring deep within the flower field. The primeval qi it produced was so concentrated and abundant that it generated this pressure. But soon, a cluster of lights rose from the flowers beside Fang Yuan’s feet. The lights drifted around him, enveloping his entire body, before every last one entered it.

“These are Hope Gu,” Fang Yuan murmured inwardly. The elder in charge had not explained, but Fang Yuan knew perfectly well.

Every point of light was an individual Gu.

Their name was Hope.

One of the oldest legends told of Hope Gu. According to legend, when this world had just formed, it was a wilderness where wild beasts roamed unchecked. The first human came into being, and his name was Ren Zu. He lived a primitive existence, eating raw flesh and drinking blood. Life was exceedingly difficult. In particular, there was a group of wild beasts called Predicaments. They found Ren Zu’s scent especially enticing and wanted to devour him. Ren Zu had neither a body as tough as mountain rock nor the sharp teeth and claws of wild beasts. How could he contend with this pack of beasts called Predicaments? His food supply was unreliable, and he spent his days hiding from place to place. At the lower end of nature’s food chain, he was almost at the point where he could no longer survive.

At this time, three Gu sought out Ren Zu of their own accord and said, “As long as you sustain us with your life, we will help you overcome your difficulties.” With nowhere else to turn, Ren Zu had no choice but to agree to the three Gu’s terms. First, he sustained the largest of the three Gu with his youth. That Gu gave him strength. With strength, Ren Zu’s life began to improve. He gained a reliable source of food and the strength to protect himself. He took eagerly to fighting and defeated many Predicaments. But he soon suffered for it. Eventually, he discovered that strength was not all-powerful: it required rest and recovery and could not be squandered at will.

Moreover, his strength alone was far too little against all the Predicament beasts together. Once his suffering had eased, Ren Zu reflected on what he had endured. He decided to sustain the most beautiful of the three Gu with his middle years, when he was in the prime of his strength. Then the second Gu gave him wisdom. With wisdom, Ren Zu learned to think and reflect on his actions, and began accumulating experience. He discovered that wisdom was often more effective than strength. With both, he was able, for a time, to overcome much that had previously been beyond him and kill more Predicaments. He ate their flesh and drank their blood, tenaciously surviving in this way. But the good times did not last. Ren Zu grew old, older and older. This was because he had offered his youth and middle years to Strength Gu and Wisdom Gu.

Once a person grows old, their muscles waste away and their mind no longer works as quickly.

“Human, what can you still give us? You have nothing left to offer us.” Once Strength Gu and Wisdom Gu realized this, they both abandoned him without compassion.

Ren Zu had lost strength and wisdom. The Predicaments found him again and surrounded him. He was old and could no longer run. All his teeth had fallen out; he could not even chew wild fruit or edible greens. He collapsed helplessly to the ground, Predicaments crowding all around him. His heart was filled with despair. Just then, the third Gu said, “Human, sustain me, and I can free you from these Predicaments.”

Ren Zu said through his tears, “Gu, what could I possibly have left? Look, Strength Gu and Wisdom Gu have both abandoned me. All I have left is my old age. Compared with youth and middle age, it may be worth little, but if I offer even that to you, my life will end immediately. Though the Predicaments surround me, I will not die just yet. I want to live a little longer, even if only for a single second. So go. I can no longer sustain you.” But the Gu said, “Of the three of us, I ask for the least. Human, all you need to do is give me your heart.”

“Then I’ll give you my heart,” Ren Zu said. “But, Gu, what can you give me? In this desperate situation, even if Strength Gu and Wisdom Gu returned to my side, they could not change anything.”

Beside Strength Gu, this Gu had the frailest body: nothing more than a tiny point of light. Beside Wisdom Gu, it was the dimmest, giving off only a faint white glow, without any splendor or brilliance. But when the human gave his heart to the Gu, it suddenly blazed with boundless light. Amid that light, the Predicaments cried out in terror: “It’s Hope Gu! Retreat! We Predicaments fear hope most of all!” The Predicament beasts immediately retreated in panic.

Ren Zu stared, eyes wide and mouth agape. From that moment on, he understood: when facing predicaments, give your heart to hope. Meanwhile, the Hope Gu had gathered into a stream of light and entered Fang Yuan’s body. Under the external pressure, they quickly gathered in Fang Yuan’s abdomen, clustering of their own accord roughly ten centimeters below his navel. Fang Yuan immediately felt the pressure ease slightly. He stepped forward and continued on. With every step he took, more Hope Gu rose from the flower field, entered his body, and joined the mass of light.

The mass of light grew larger and brighter. But across the river, the elder in charge frowned.

“There seem to be rather few Hope Gu.” At the sight, the hearts of the many elders who had been discreetly watching Fang Yuan sank.

The Clan Leader frowned as well. These were certainly not the signs one would expect from Grade A aptitude! Fang Yuan continued forward against the pressure.

“Fewer than ten steps means no aptitude for cultivation. Ten to twenty steps means Grade D; twenty to thirty, Grade C; thirty to forty, Grade B; forty to fifty, Grade A. So far, I have taken twenty-three steps.” “Twenty-four, twenty-five, twenty-six… twenty-seven.” Fang Yuan counted silently. At his twenty-seventh step, he seemed to hear a boom: the mass of light in his abdomen, between his kidneys, had reached its limit and suddenly burst.

The burst occurred entirely within Fang Yuan’s body; no one else could detect it. Only he experienced its mysterious, earth-shaking wonder in that instant. In an instant, the fine hairs all over his body stood on end and his pores closed tightly. His mind drew taut like a bowstring suddenly pulled back. Immediately afterward, his mind went blank and his whole body relaxed, as though he had fallen into the clouds. The tension within him eased, his body hair settled, and every pore opened. All at once, a light sheen of perspiration covered his body.

Though it takes some time to describe, the entire process was exceedingly brief. It came swiftly and ended just as abruptly. Fang Yuan was dazed for only an instant before collecting himself. He quietly concentrated and examined within his body. An aperture had appeared out of nowhere in his abdomen, below his navel and between his kidneys.

His aperture had opened successfully!

This was the hope of immortality!

## Chapter 6
The aperture was a thing of surpassing mystery. Though it was lodged within Fang Yuan’s body, it did not share the same space as his internal organs. You could call it infinitely vast, and you could just as well call it infinitely small. Some called it the Purple Palace. Others called it the Flowery Pool. Most, however, called it the Primeval Sea Aperture. This was what the Hope Gu had become: burst apart, then drawn together into a single layer of light. It was precisely this membrane of light that held the aperture together and kept it from collapsing.

Within the aperture, naturally, lay the primeval sea. The water lay smooth as a mirror, a sheet of jade green, yet viscous beyond any water, and it carried the sheen of bronze. This was the bronze primeval essence that only a Rank 1 Gu Master possessed, condensed together. Commonly, it was called the bronze sea. The surface of the sea did not reach half the height of the aperture. It stood at only forty-four percent. This too was the limit of a Grade C aptitude. Every drop of that water was primeval essence. It represented the condensing of Fang Yuan’s essence, qi and spirit, and it stood for the vital potential he had accumulated over fifteen years.

It was with this primeval essence that a Gu Master drove his Gu worms. Which was to say, from this moment on, Fang Yuan had formally stepped into the ranks of Rank 1 Gu Masters. The aperture was open. No more Hope Gu flowed into Fang Yuan’s body. Fang Yuan withdrew his attention. He felt only that the pressure ahead was as thick and solid as a wall, and that he could not advance another step.

“Just as in my previous life.” He gave a faint, indifferent smile at the result.

“Can you go no further?” The academy elder called from across the river, still clinging to that one chance in ten thousand.

Fang Yuan simply turned and walked back, answering him by action. At that, even the youths finally caught on. At once there was a rising buzz, and the crowd erupted.

“What? Fang Yuan walked twenty-seven steps?”

“So he only has Grade C aptitude?!”

“Unbelievable. A genius like him, only of Grade C?”

An uproar swept through the crowd.

“Brother…” In the crowd, Gu Yue Fang Zheng raised his head and stared in shock at Fang Yuan wading back across the river. He could not believe the scene before him. His own elder brother was only of Grade C?

He had always believed his elder brother would be of Grade A aptitude. No, not only he. His uncle and aunt, and many others in the clan, had believed the same. And yet now, this was the result!

“Damn it. Only of Grade C!” The Gu Yue clan leader clenched his fists in secret and let out a deep sigh. His disappointment was plain beyond any words.

Among the elders watching in secret, some knitted their brows, some lowered their heads in discussion, and some tipped their heads back and let out a long sigh.

“Could there be an error in the test?”

“How could it be? The method is accurate beyond compare, and with us watching throughout, even cheating would be difficult.”

“But then how do you explain his earlier performance, and his talent?”

“The higher a youth’s primeval sea aptitude, the more he will indeed display qualities beyond the ordinary. Intelligence, comprehension, memory, strength, agility and so on. But the reverse does not hold. Those qualities do not mean the aptitude must be high. In the end, the test result is what decides.”

“Haa. The greater the hope, the greater the disappointment. The Gu Yue clan grows worse with every generation.”

* * *

The icy river water soaked through his socks, cold enough to bite at the bone. Fang Yuan walked on, his face as expressionless as ever. The distance closed, and he could see clearly the heavy look on the academy elder’s face, and could feel keenly the eyes of more than a hundred youths fixed on him. In those eyes there was astonishment, there was shock, there was mockery, there was delight at his misfortune, there was sudden understanding, and there was cold indifference. The scene was identical, and it drew Fang Yuan’s thoughts back to his previous life.

Back then he had felt as if the sky had come down. He had missed his footing crossing the river and gone sprawling, soaked through and out of his wits. And not one person had put out a hand to him. Those disappointed, indifferent faces and eyes had been like sharp knives slicing his heart away piece by piece. His thoughts had run wild, and a dull ache had spread through his chest. It was like falling from the clouds to the ground. The higher one stood, the harder one fell.

But in this life, standing once more in such a scene, Fang Yuan was utterly calm. He thought of that legend. When predicaments come, entrust your heart to hope. And now that hope lay within his own body. It was not a great hope, but it was already better than those with no aptitude for cultivation at all. If others were disappointed by it, then let them be disappointed. What of it? What is the disappointment of others to me? What matters is that one keeps hope in one’s own heart!

Five hundred years of living had taught him one truth. What makes a life interesting is the pursuit of one’s own dreams. There is no need to demand that others withhold their disappointment, or grant their approval.

Go your own way, and let others be disappointed, let them disapprove!

“Haa…” The academy elder let out a deep sigh, then called again. “Next. Gu Yue Fang Zheng.”

No one answered.

“Gu Yue Fang Zheng!” The elder shouted, and his voice went humming and echoing through the karst cavern.

“Ah? Here, I’m here!” Fang Zheng tore himself free of his shock and hurried forward, only to stagger underfoot. He went straight over in a tumble and fell with a splash, rolling, as luck would have it, right into the river.

At once, laughter broke out across the whole cavern.

“The Fang brothers. Nothing more than this.” The Gu Yue clan leader gave a cold snort, and a certain distaste for Gu Yue Fang Zheng rose in him as well.

“Now I’ve really disgraced myself!” Fang Zheng thrashed hard a few times in the water, but he could not get to his feet at all, for the riverbed was slick. All his effort only made him look clumsier. Burst after burst of laughter reached his ears, and he grew more flustered still.

But just then he felt a great force yank at him, and his head came up out of the water at last. His body found its balance. He wiped his face in a sorry state and looked and saw that it was his elder brother Fang Yuan, gripping him by the collar and hauling him up.

“Brother…” He opened his mouth to speak and only choked on water, which set off a violent fit of coughing.

Soon, a week had gone by.

"Humans are the spirit of all living things, Gu are the essence of heaven and earth. In this world there exist thousands upon thousands of countless kinds of Gu. They live all around us, in ore and soil, in thickets of grass, and even inside the bodies of wild beasts."

Inside the academy of the village, the academy elder was speaking with calm assurance and fluent ease.

Facing him, fifty-seven youths sat upright, each one listening with rapt attention.

The wonder and power of Gu Masters had long since sunk deep into the hearts of the youths. Therefore everything the elder told them drew them in deeply.

At that moment, a youth raised his hand. After receiving the elder's permission, he stood and asked, "Lord Academy Elder, I have known since I was very small that Gu Masters are divided into Rank 1, Rank 2, and so on. Could you explain it to us in detail?"

Elder Gu Yue nodded and waved his hand for the youth to sit down. "Gu Masters have nine great realms in total. From lowest to highest, they are Rank 1, Rank 2, Rank 3, all the way to Rank 9. Within each great realm there are four small realms, initial stage, middle stage, upper stage, and peak stage. You have just become Gu Masters, so you are all at initial stage Rank 1."

"If you cultivate diligently from now on, your cultivation will naturally rise, and advancing to Rank 2 or Rank 3 is also possible. Of course, the higher your aptitude, the greater the possibility of advancement."

"With Grade D aptitude, the primeval sea occupies twenty to thirty percent of the aperture. Such a person can often cultivate at most to Rank 1 or Rank 2. With Grade C aptitude, the primeval sea is forty to fifty percent of the aperture. Such people usually stop at Rank 2, and only a very small number can luckily break through to initial stage Rank 3. With Grade B aptitude, the primeval sea occupies sixty to seventy percent of the whole aperture. They can cultivate to Rank 3, even Rank 4. With Grade A aptitude, the primeval sea is abundant, eighty to ninety percent of the aperture. Naturally such people have the highest talent, are the most suited to Gu Master cultivation, and can cultivate to Rank 5."

"As for Gu Masters of Rank 6 and above, each one is a legend. I am not too clear on the specifics myself. Our Gu Yue clan has never produced a Rank 6 Gu Master, but we have had Rank 4 and Rank 5 Gu Masters before."

The youths all pricked up their ears and listened with eyes shining bright.

Many could not help looking toward Gu Yue Fang Zheng, who sat bolt upright in the first row. He had Grade A aptitude after all, and their gazes were filled without exception with envy and jealousy.

At the same time, a portion of gazes drifted toward that corner in the very last row of the academy.

In that corner by the window, Gu Yue Fang Yuan was sprawled over his desk, fast asleep and snoring.

"Ah, the blow was really too great. He carried the name of genius for so many years, who would have thought that in the end he would only be Grade C, heh heh."

Hearing the discussion by his ears grow louder and louder, the academy elder's brows had already knotted into a lump.

In the whole classroom, the youths all sat upright and radiated youthful vigor, which made Fang Yuan, sprawled asleep on his desk, stand out even more glaringly.

"A whole week has passed, and he is still so dispirited. Humph, I misjudged him back then. How could such a person possibly be a genius!" The elder snorted coldly with displeasure in his heart. He had already spoken to Fang Yuan about this situation many times. But it had no effect at all. Fang Yuan continued to do as he pleased. ## Chapter 8
# Chapter 8: Things Remain, People Have Changed
## 第八节：物是人非

Beside the academy stood a Gu chamber. The chamber was not large, only sixty square metres.

In the cultivation of a Gu Master, Gu worms are the ultimate key to strength.

As soon as lessons ended, the excited youths swarmed toward the Gu chamber.

"Queue up, come in one by one!" A stern shout rang out. Guards were naturally stationed outside the Gu chamber door.

The youths went in one by one, and then came out.

It was Fang Yuan's turn to enter.

Inside, he saw that there was far more to the room than met the eye. The four walls were carved into wall recesses, these inset square compartments crowding one after another. They varied in size, the largest no bigger than a clay pot, and the smallest no larger than a fist.

Within the tightly packed compartments sat a vast array of vessels: grey stone basins, vibrant green jade dishes, delicate grass cages, and earthenware braziers.

In these vessels were kept all kinds of Gu worms.

Some Gu worms were quiescent and mute, while others were clamorous, emanating a din of chirps, clucks, and rustles that coalesced into a symphony of life.

"Gu worms are likewise divided into nine major ranks, corresponding to the nine ranks of Gu Masters. The Gu worms here are all Rank I." Fang Yuan surveyed the chamber, comprehending at a single glance.

Generally, a Rank I Gu Master can only use Rank I Gu worms. Should they attempt to activate a higher-ranked Gu worm exceeding their current rank, they will invariably incur a devastating toll.

Moreover, Gu worms require constant sustenance; the toll of nurturing a superior Gu worm is frequently far more than an inferior Gu Master can bear.

Barring exceptional circumstances, novice Gu Masters invariably select a Rank I Gu worm for their first refinement.

The very first Gu worm a Gu Master refines carries great significance; known as their vital Gu, it is inextricably bound to their life force. Should it perish, the Gu Master will inevitably suffer devastating damage.

"Alas, I had originally hoped to obtain the Flower Wine Monk's Liquor Worm and refine it as my vital Gu. But now, the search for the Flower Wine Monk's remains still yields no clues; there is no telling when I might find them, or if someone else will discover them first. To be safe, I should still choose a Moonlight Gu." 

Fang Yuan sighed inwardly even as he walked straight towards the wall on his left.

Slightly higher up among those wall recesses was a row of silver plates. Upon each plate rested a Gu worm.

This Gu worm was crystal clear and curved like a crescent moon, resembling a piece of blue crystal. Set against the silver base of the plate, it radiated a serene, ethereal aura.

Its name is Moonlight, the guardian Gu worm of the Gu Yue clan, chosen by the vast majority as their vital Gu. It is no wild Gu worm, but one cultivated through the Gu Yue clan's secret methods and found nowhere else. It can be said to be the very emblem of the Gu Yue clan.

All were Rank I Moonlight Gu, differences extremely subtle. Fang Yuan casually picked one and held it in his hand.

Moonlight Gu was very light, comparable to the weight of a thin sheet of paper. It only occupied a small patch of his palm, about the size of a common jade pendant. Held in his hand, he could see through it the lines of his palm beneath.

After a final glance confirming nothing was amiss, Fang Yuan put it in his pocket and left the Gu chamber.

Outside the chamber a long queue still stretched; the next youth, seeing Fang Yuan come out, hurried excitedly into the chamber.

If it were anyone else, upon obtaining a Gu worm, the first reaction would be to rush home to refine it quickly. But Fang Yuan did not hurry to do so; his heart still lingered on that Liquor Worm.

Liquor Worm was more precious; although Moonlight Gu was a specialty of Gu Yue Village, it did not help a Gu Master as much as Liquor Worm.

Leaving the Gu chamber, Fang Yuan went directly to the wine shop.

"Shopkeeper, two jars of aged wine." Fang Yuan fished in his pocket and placed the remaining fragments of primeval stones on the counter.

These past days, he would come here to buy wine, then wander around the outskirts of the village searching, intending to lure that Liquor Worm to appear.

The shopkeeper was a short, middle-aged fat man, his face oily; after these days he had already remembered Fang Yuan.

"Honoured customer, you're here." While greeting, he stretched out his thick, short, fat hand and deftly swept away Fang Yuan's primeval stone fragments.

Then he weighed them in his hand, feeling the weight was not lacking, the shopkeeper's smile grew a bit more cordial.

Primeval stones are the currency of this world, used to measure the value of all commodities. At the same time they are the condensation of heaven and earth's essence, usable in themselves, and important in helping a Gu Master in cultivation.

Having both monetary and commodity attributes, they are extremely similar to gold on Earth. Earth had a single gold standard system; in this world it is a single primeval stone standard system.

Compared to gold, the purchasing power of primeval stones is even more astonishing.

However, no matter how many primeval stones one had, they could not withstand Fang Yuan's continuous consumption like this.

"Two jars of wine every day, for seven days straight. The primeval stones I had saved up are almost all spent." Carrying two jars out of the shop, Fang Yuan's brows furrowed slightly.

Once one becomes a Gu Master, one can directly extract pure primeval essence from a primeval stone to replenish the primeval sea in the aperture.

Therefore for Gu Masters, primeval stones are not only currency, but also an aid in cultivation.

With sufficient primeval stones, cultivation speed can increase greatly; this can more or less make up for some shortcomings in aptitude.

"Tomorrow I will have no primeval stones to buy wine. Yet the Liquor Worm still shows no sign of itself. Must I really refine Moonlight Gu as my vital Gu?" Fang Yuan felt somewhat unwilling in his heart.

Leaving the wine shop, Fang Yuan carried two jars of wine, walking while pondering: "The academy elder said, the first person to refine vital Gu in this assessment will be rewarded with twenty primeval stones. Right now I guess many are at home mustering all their energy, refining Gu worms, vying for first place. Alas, refining vital Gu extremely tests aptitude. Those with good aptitude have huge advantage. With my Grade C aptitude, without other means, I fundamentally have no hope of winning."

Just then, Gu Yue Fang Zheng's voice sounded behind him: "Brother, you really came to the wine shop to get drunk again! Come with me, uncle and aunt want to see you."

Fang Yuan stopped and turned.

He found his younger brother no longer spoke with his head lowered as before.

The brothers' gazes collided.

A gust of wind blew, lifting the elder brother's messy black hair and fluttering the younger brother's robes.

A mere month, yet things had remained while people had changed.

A week ago, the Aperture Opening Ceremony was a huge change for both elder and younger brother.

Elder brother Fang Yuan fell from the clouds, the halo of genius ruthlessly stripped away. Younger brother began to shine, like a new star, slowly rising.

This change, for younger brother Gu Yue Fang Zheng, held a meaning of earth-shaking overturn.

He finally tasted the feeling his brother once had — being entrusted with hopes, being watched with envious or jealous gazes.

He felt as though he had suddenly moved from a gloomy corner into a heaven filled with light.

Every day waking, he felt as if he were living a beautiful dream. The vastly different treatment still left him somewhat disbelieving, and at the same time strongly uncomfortable.

Unaccustomed.

Suddenly from being unknown, to being closely watched, pointed at and discussed.

Sometimes Fang Zheng walking on the road, hearing passers-by discuss him, praising him, would feel his face burn, hands and feet at a loss, eyes evading, almost not knowing how to walk!

For the first ten-odd days, Gu Yue Fang Zheng inexplicably lost weight, but his spirit grew more vigorous.

From the deepest part of his heart, something called "confidence" began to grow.

"So this is what brother used to feel, truly wonderful and painful!" He could not help thinking of his elder brother Gu Yue Fang Yuan — facing such discussion and attention, how did brother handle it before?

He subconsciously began to imitate Fang Yuan, pretending to be expressionless, but quickly found he was not cut out for it.

Sometimes in the academy, a girl's shout could make him blush furiously. On the road, the teasing of aunties made him flee in disarray many times.

He was like an infant learning to walk, stumbling as he adapted to new life.

In this process, he inevitably heard rumours about his brother — dejected and decadent, turned to alcohol, not returning home at night, sleeping heavily in academy.

At first he was extremely shocked — his brother, that powerful, genius existence, had become like this?!

But gradually, he began to somewhat understand. Brother is also an ordinary person; encountering such setback and blow, dejection is inevitable.

Accompanying this understanding, Fang Zheng faintly felt a burst of indescribable pleasure.

This pleasurable emotion was something he extremely did not want to admit, but it did exist.

The elder brother praised as genius, who had once suppressed him like a shadow, now so downfallen and dejected. From the opposite side, it all the more witnessed his own growth, did it not?

I am outstanding, that is the truth!

Therefore, seeing Fang Yuan carrying wine jars, hair messy, clothes untidy, Gu Yue Fang Zheng's heart fiercely exhaled, breathing inexplicably much easier.

But his mouth said: "Brother, you cannot drink again, cannot go on like this. Don't you know how worried those who care about you will be? You must pull yourself together!"

Fang Yuan remained expressionless, saying nothing.

The brothers looked at each other.

In younger brother Gu Yue Fang Zheng's eyes shone bright, revealing a sharp intent. While elder brother Fang Yuan's eyes were deep and dark, like an ancient, secluded pool.

Such eyes made Fang Zheng feel an inexplicable pressure. Not long into the stare, he subconsciously shifted his gaze to the side.

But when he realised what he had done, a surge of anger rose in his heart.

An anger toward himself.

What's wrong with me? Don't even have courage to look brother in the eye?

I have changed, I have completely changed!

Thinking this, his gaze sharpened again and swept back.

But Fang Yuan no longer looked at him, but carrying a jar of wine in each hand, walked past him, his flat voice drifting: "What are you dazed for, let's go."

Fang Zheng's breathing disordered, the breath he had built up in his chest now had nowhere to vent, leaving him with an inexpressible gloom.

Seeing elder brother already walking far, he had no choice but to quickly follow.

Only this time, his head was no longer lowered, but raised facing the sunset.

His gaze watched his own feet, stepping step by step onto his brother Fang Yuan's shadow.
